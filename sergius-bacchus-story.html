Sergius and Bacchus: Two Friends Who Stood Together

Meet two friends in the emperor's guard who made one hard choice together. Read together, then listen for the friends beside you.

Short answer: This nine-page storybook for ages 6 to 12 follows Saints Sergius and Bacchus, two Roman officers around the year 300, from the emperor's palace to the desert city that took Sergius's name. It ends with a narrated family reflection to share together.

Story Summary

Sergius and Bacchus were Roman officers and close friends. The old account tells how they stayed faithful to Jesus together. Nine narrative pages end with a narrated family reflection.

    About this story

    Illustrations are imaginative composites, not eyewitness scenes. The full account of these two saints is an old Greek Passion, probably written in the 5th century, and historians treat many details as legend, so the story says "the old account" where it follows that text. Sources disagree on which emperor was involved, so the story names none. The hard parts of the story are told gently on purpose.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Who were Saints Sergius and Bacchus?

    Two Roman army officers around the year 300 who stayed faithful to Jesus. They are remembered as martyrs, and called Mar Sarkis and Bakhos in Arabic. Their feast is 7 October.

    Is the story true?

    Their martyrdom is old and widely honored. The details come from a 5th-century account that historians treat partly as legend, so the story tells those parts as "the old account".

    Who is this storybook for?

    Children ages 6 to 12, reading or listening with family. Nine illustrated pages lead to a four-part family reflection.

# Marsharbel Viral Video Playbook

Version 1 - compiled 2026-11-18 from public sources. Every claim is tagged [official] (the platform's own docs/blog), [study] (measured data, cited), [academic] (peer-reviewed), or [opinion] (practitioner advice, treat as hypothesis).

## How the video lanes use this file

1. Read this file before building any video (rosary, saint story, reel, Short).
2. Apply the Build Checklist (section 9) to the script, edit, thumbnail and caption.
3. After every release, append one entry to the Lessons Log (section 10) with the real numbers.
4. When the Lessons Log contradicts something in here, the log wins for our audience, and the contradiction gets noted in the entry.

## TL;DR - the ten rules

1. The first 2-3 seconds decide distribution. Sound and speech from frame one, no cold open, no logo intro. [study]
2. Open on the strongest shot and the strongest sentence. Cut the windup, start on the payoff line. [study]
3. Put the hook in writing on the first frame: about 4-7 words, readable, not a giant title card. On-screen hooks roughly doubled views vs spoken-only in one 1,272-video dataset. [study]
4. Thumbnail + title are the billboard for long-form: 90% of best-performing videos have custom thumbnails; titles accurate, important words first. [official]
5. Retention is the ranking fuel: viewers staying past the opening is what gets a video pushed to bigger audiences. [official] [study]
6. Cut the dead air: big creators change shots every 2-3 seconds after the opening. Uncut single takes underperformed. [study]
7. End with a recap, not "let me know below". Soft-invite endings went with 39% fewer views; recaps with 52% more. [study]
8. High-arousal emotion is what people share. For us that means awe, wonder, hope, gratitude - not outrage or shock. [academic]
9. Publish time does not matter for long-term performance; consistency and quality do. [official]
10. Breakouts are normal if you keep posting: the typical account's best recent video drew 42x its median. Volume plus iteration is the strategy. [study]

## 1. How distribution actually works

### Long-form YouTube [official]

YouTube states its recommendation system has two goals: help each viewer find videos they want to watch, and maximize long-term viewer satisfaction. The signals it names: clicks, watch time, satisfaction survey responses ("valued watchtime" - only 4-5 star ratings count), shares, likes, dislikes. Recommendations drive more viewership than subscriptions or search. The system compares a viewer's habits with similar viewers ("automated word-of-mouth").

Official growth guidance from the same page: consistent quality over upload frequency, publish time is not known to affect long-term performance, series + playlists + end screens help viewers watch more, and one failed experiment does not hurt the channel because each video gets fresh performance data.

Sources: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387 and https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/

### Shorts (and by extension Reels/TikTok feeds) [official + secondary]

There is no click in a feed - the viewer's only choices are watch or swipe. So the algorithm tests every Short on a small "seed audience" first (explore), then pushes it wider only if that group holds (exploit). This is why a Short can stall at a few hundred views: the seed audience swiped.

Since 2025-03-31 every Short that starts or replays counts as a view; since 2026-08-24 "engaged view" (watching past the initial seconds) is the metric that feeds analytics, recommendations and monetization. Watch "Viewed vs swiped away" and retention in Studio, not raw views.

Sources: https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/ and https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/ (secondary, includes YouTube product lead quotes).

## 2. The hook: first seconds decide everything

### What the platforms measure [official, via ReelsBot's sourcing]

- Instagram replaced view rate with skip rate: the share who skip in the first 3 seconds (Aug 2025).
- TikTok Creator Rewards does not pay for views under 5 seconds.
- YouTube Shorts "stayed to watch" means past "the initial seconds" - YouTube has never published the number. Long-form "intro" metric = share still watching after 30 seconds.

### Measured viewer behavior [study: ReelsBot, 2026-10-30]

Analysis of 2.2M Kuaishou plays (2022) and 33.6M VK plays (2025) - not TikTok/Shorts/Reels, so treat as directional:

- Recommended videos: 67.9% still watching at 3s, 46% at 10s.
- Cold (randomly inserted) videos: only 37.2% still watching at 3s; median play 2.1s. This is the closest public data to a stranger meeting a video cold.
- The most common swipe moment is 1.2-1.3 seconds in.
- Videos that held strangers past 3s got 2.7x the impressions of the worst holders (correlation, rho = 0.28). The 7-second hold tracked reach even better (rho = 0.37).

Source: https://reelsbot.co/blog/first-three-seconds

### What top creators actually do in the opening [study: Shortform Kit, 2026-11-17]

167 newest videos from 21 of the largest Reels/TikTok/Shorts creators:

- Median first cut at 2.6s - only 14% cut within the first second. One strong shot holds while the first sentence lands.
- 84% of spoken videos have speech within the first 2 seconds; 95% have sound from the first frame.
- 58% show text on the first frame (hook line or title).
- After the opening: median shot length 2.3s, about 25 cuts per minute.
- Median video length 36-40s across the three apps.

Source: https://shortformkit.com/short-form-video-hook-study-2026/

### Which hooks correlate with views [study: Postdom, 2026-10-05]

1,272 TikToks/Reels from 160 accounts; each video compared against its own account's median (directional multipliers - the sample oversampled each account's best and worst videos, so trust direction and ranking, not exact sizes):

- Hook written on screen: 1.79x views. Spoken-only hook: 0.62x. Little/no on-screen text: 0.46x.
- Hook length made no clear difference (median 7 words). ALL CAPS hooks: 0.75x. Giant title-card text: 0.38x.
- Hook opens with a role ("as a nurse..."): 3.16x. First-person story hook: 1.74x. Open loop in the hook: 1.46x.
- First frame is a real place: 2.28x. Product/object close-up: 0.56x. Account's usual presenter on screen: 1.40x; a different person: 0.69x.

Source: https://postdom.com/research/what-makes-a-video-go-viral

### Writing the hook [study: ScaleReach, 2026-10-01 - vendor data, weighs its own bias]

4,843 clips: the spoken hook is a cut, not a line you write - start the clip on the sentence that carries the payoff, not the setup. On-screen title spec their system uses: 4-5 words, max 30 characters, shown for the first 3 seconds, statement not question, name the stakes not the topic ("The Startup Valuation Trap" beats "Startup Valuations"). Most common fixes: cut filler (41% of suggestions), fix the ending (15%), start at a different line (13%). Hook types their AI detects most: surprise/reveal (21%), a number or statistic (12%), contrarian claim (9%), relatable situation (7%), story/anecdote (5%).

Source: https://www.scalereach.ai/blog/how-to-write-a-hook-for-short-videos/

## 3. Retention: the ranking fuel

[official] YouTube's key-moments report: intro (share still watching at 30s), top moments, spikes (rewatched/shared), dips (skipped/abandoned). High CTR with low retention means the thumbnail/title promised something the video did not deliver - fix the promise or the opening, not the topic. Move the compelling content earlier; expand top moments into new videos.
Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415

[study: Postdom] Editing pace: 10-25 cuts per minute went with 1.53x views; one-take no-cut videos 0.69x. Listicle format 2.12x; story format 1.50x.

[opinion, vidIQ] Remove slow intros, unnecessary pauses and delayed payoffs; give viewers a reason to stay to the end because stronger retention signals the Short satisfied its matched audience.

## 4. Why people share: arousal, not valence [academic]

Berger (Wharton, Psychological Science 2011), two experiments: people induced into high-arousal emotions (amusement, anxiety) shared neutral content significantly more than people in low-arousal states (contentment, sadness). Even jogging in place for 60 seconds raised sharing of an unrelated article from 33% to 75%. Positive vs negative mattered less than activated vs deactivated.

Source: https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/07/Arousal2.pdf

Marsharbel implication [our interpretation, not from the paper]: devotional content does not need shock or outrage to be shareable. Awe (a miracle account, a healing testimony), hope (a prayer answered), and reverent wonder are high-arousal positive states. Sadness-only framings (pure mourning, slow lament) are low-arousal and predict less sharing. Frame the same true story around wonder and gratitude.

## 5. Thumbnails and titles (long-form) [official]

- 90% of best-performing videos have custom thumbnails.
- Thumbnails: simple composition, readable text, emotion or action for casual viewers, familiar visuals for subscribers; refresh old thumbnails freely (does not affect views/data).
- Titles: accurate first (misleading titles kill retention and discoverability), important words near the front, episode numbers and branding at the end, limited caps/emoji. Searchable titles for search-driven topics, intriguing titles for browse.
- Use Test & Compare (A/B thumbnails) even on videos public for months.
- CTR is a percentage: when a video goes viral CTR drops as impressions explode - judge by views, not CTR alone. Check CTR on Home/Suggested in the first 24h for general audiences, Subs feed for fans.

Sources: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300 and https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/master-these-4-metrics/

## 6. Endings, captions and CTAs [study: Postdom]

- End with a summary/recap: 1.52x views, 1.21x saves.
- "Let me know below" soft invite: 0.61x views (the most common ending - 30% of videos - and a consistent loser; holds on TikTok alone at 0.63x and in the design-robust check).
- Ending that asks for a follow: 2.11x views (range wide, not statistically clear) but 1.57x saves and 1.39x shares - the clearest CTA winner.
- Hard sell (link/buy/book/DM): 0.94x - no penalty, no lift.
- Caption ending in a question: 0.17x views (small n=23, flag as fragile). Caption under 60 characters: 0.60x.

## 7. Length, volume and cadence

- [study: Postdom] No length bracket reliably changed views. TikToks over 90s earned 1.29x saves and 1.26x shares per view. Length is a depth/leverage choice, not a views lever.
- [secondary: Nate Black's 35B-views Shorts research via vidIQ] Shorts of ~13s or ~60s tended to perform best; channels past 200 published Shorts tend to see consistent view growth. Treat as practitioner finding, not platform word.
- [study: Postdom] Breakouts are normal: the typical account's best recent video drew 42x its median; 84% of accounts had at least one 10x video. You cannot predict which one breaks out; you can only keep shipping well-built attempts.
- [official] Publish time is not known to affect long-term performance (post for audience-active hours only for premieres/live). Do not burn production quality to hit a clock.

## 8. What to measure after every release [official]

From YouTube Studio (the 4 metrics YouTube itself tells creators to master):

1. CTR (first 24h, Home/Suggested for new viewers; Subs feed for fans) - judges thumbnail + title.
2. Audience retention / key moments - judges the video itself: intro %, dips, spikes, swiped-away ratio for Shorts.
3. Traffic sources - where views came from; external embeds drag retention averages down without meaning the video failed.
4. Videos growing your audience (Audience tab, 90 days) - which videos act as the front door; make more like those and build series off them.

Source: https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/master-these-4-metrics/

## 9. Marsharbel build checklist

Apply to every rosary video, saint story, reel and Short before publish:

- [ ] Frame one has sound and (if narrated) speech. No silent fade-in, no logo card first. Our candle + Saint Charbel template opens the shot - it is the "usual presenter" (1.40x) and should carry motion (flame) from the first frame.
- [ ] The first spoken line is the payoff (the miracle, the promise of the prayer, the saint's most striking moment), not "welcome back to...". Cut every second of windup.
- [ ] One short on-screen hook line on the first frame, 4-7 words, normal case, medium size, inside the app safe zones. Names the stakes: "The night Saint Charbel's Mass ended" not "Saint Charbel part 5".
- [ ] After the opening, a visual change every 2-3 seconds (art pan/cut, scene change). No static shot past ~3 seconds.
- [ ] Story or list structure. No uncut single takes.
- [ ] Emotion target: awe / hope / gratitude / wonder. Never manufactured outrage or fear; reverence is the brand. (Awe is high-arousal - it shares.)
- [ ] Ending: 2-3 second recap of the one thing to remember, then the subscribe/follow ask ("subscribe to support the channel and help us create more videos like this" - Joey's standing line; Postdom: follow-ask endings went with the most saves and shares), then the marsharbel.com end splash, super visible (Joey's hard rule).
- [ ] Never end with "let me know below". The comment-PRAYER trigger stays as an on-screen/caption element mid-video, not as the ending line. Do not end the caption with a question mark.
- [ ] Caption: 60+ characters, states what the video gives, no trailing question.
- [ ] Long-form only: custom thumbnail (candle + portrait + <=4 words), title accurate with keywords early, A/B test via Test & Compare after 24-48h if CTR lags.
- [ ] Cross-post the same edit to Shorts/Reels/TikTok - 47%+ of top creators post one edit everywhere, and the openings measured nearly identical across apps.

## Known caveats

- Postdom multipliers are inflated by design (best/worst oversampling) and observational (correlation, not cause). Use direction and ranking only.
- Kuaishou/VK play-log behavior may not transfer exactly to Shorts/Reels/TikTok.
- Platform rules change: re-verify the official pages quarterly (set a review wake each quarter).
- Nothing here overrides Joey's standing rules (candle + portrait template, marsharbel.com splash, subscribe ask, reverence, AI-rendition labeling).

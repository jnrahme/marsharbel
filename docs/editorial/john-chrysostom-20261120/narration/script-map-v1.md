## Page 01 - Enter the world
Art: books-src/john-chrysostom/art/page-01.webp (present)

Around the year 349, in Antioch, a great city of the ancient world, a boy named John was born. His father had died, so his mother Anthusa raised him alone, and she raised him in the faith.

John grew up in a city full of markets, theaters and noise. Nobody guessed the quiet boy would become the most famous preacher the early Church ever had.

## Page 02 - Let us care
Art: books-src/john-chrysostom/art/page-02.webp (present)

John was a brilliant student. He studied under Libanius, the most famous teacher of the age, and everyone expected him to become a great lawyer. Instead, John chose the Church. He was baptized, and then he did something surprising: he went to live as a monk in the mountains outside the city.

For six years he prayed there, four years with an old teacher and two years alone in a cave, learning the Bible by heart. The hard life broke his health, and he had to come home. But the Bible was now inside him.

## Page 03 - Something changes
Art: books-src/john-chrysostom/art/page-03.webp (present)

Back in Antioch, John became a priest in the year 386. He began to preach in the city's great church, and for twelve years the city could not get enough of him.

John took the Bible verse by verse and brought it down into daily life: how to pray, how to raise children, how to treat the poor. People said listening to him was like hearing gold. Later generations gave him a new name: Chrysostom, which is Greek for golden mouth.

## Page 04 - A choice becomes action
Art: books-src/john-chrysostom/art/page-04.webp (present)

One year, a riot over taxes shook Antioch, and the whole city was afraid of being punished. While others panicked, John climbed into the pulpit. Week after week through Lent, his sermons held the frightened city steady, until the emperor's pardon finally came.

John had learned something he would never forget: words are not for showing off. Words are for serving people.

## Page 05 - Not everything is easy
Art: books-src/john-chrysostom/art/page-05.webp (present)

In the year 398, John was taken, practically against his will, to be archbishop of Constantinople, the capital of the empire. It was the wealthiest court in the world, and John did not change his sermons to please it.

He preached what he had always preached: Christ is hungry in the man at the door. At the imperial court, that sounded like an attack. Powerful people began to hate the golden mouth.

## Page 06 - Keep going
Art: books-src/john-chrysostom/art/page-06.webp (present)

John did not soften his words. He cut the spending of his own grand household and gave the money to hospitals and to the poor. He kept saying that the same Jesus is on the altar and at the door.

The powerful fought back. A gathering of bishops condemned him. The people of the city rose up for him, and for a few days he came back. But in the year 404 he was sent into exile for good, far away in the mountains. Even there, his letters traveled like sermons.

## Page 07 - The story's main moment
Art: books-src/john-chrysostom/art/page-07.webp (present)

Here is the heart of everything John preached, in his own words: "Would you honor the body of Christ? Do not neglect him when he is naked."

John explained: the one who said "This is my body" also said "I was hungry and you gave me no food." Honor Christ in church, he said, and honor him first outside the church, in the poor.

That one sentence is why the Church still reads him today.

## Page 08 - What followed
Art: books-src/john-chrysostom/art/page-08.webp (present)

John's enemies sent him deeper and deeper into exile, forcing the old, sick man to march from one remote place to the next. On September 14, 407, on the road at Comana, John died.

His disciple Palladius remembered his last words. Stripped of his city, his church and his strength, the golden mouth ended with a hymn: "Glory to God for all things."

## Page 09 - Carry the story home
Art: books-src/john-chrysostom/art/page-09.webp (present)

The story does not end there. Thirty-one years after John's death, the emperor Theodosius II, the son of the empress who had exiled him, brought John's remains back to Constantinople in triumph, and publicly asked his forgiveness.

The empire is gone. The golden words are still here. Every Sunday, millions of Christians in the Eastern churches, Catholic and Orthodox, pray the liturgy that carries John's name. Antioch, John's own city, is the mother church of the Maronites. Saint Charbel's tradition grew from the same soil.

## Page 10 - Points of reflection
Art: books-src/john-chrysostom/art/page-10.webp (present)

John said the same Jesus we meet in church is waiting for us in the poor at the door. Where could you meet Jesus outside of church this week?

Read together from the Bible: "Whatever you did for one of the least of these, you did for me." (Matthew 25:40, paraphrased.)

Today, honor Christ outside the church: share food, pocket money or time with someone who needs it.

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are in church and at the door. Open my eyes to see you in everyone I meet. Saint John Chrysostom, pray for us. Glory to God for all things. Amen.

## Page 01 - Enter the world
Art: books-src/fulton-sheen/art/page-01.webp (present)

In 1895, in the small town of El Paso, Illinois, a boy named Peter John Sheen was born into a family that loved God. His family loved him so much they gave him a funny nickname: Uncle Fultie.

Little Fultie grew up on the flat farmland of America, a world away from radio, television, and everything he would one day do.

## Page 02 - Let us care
Art: books-src/fulton-sheen/art/page-02.webp (present)

Fultie loved to learn. He studied so well that he was sent to Rome, then to Washington, then to Louvain in Belgium, one of the greatest schools in Europe. He became a priest for his home diocese of Peoria, Illinois, and then a professor and a writer of books.

But books were not enough for him. He wanted everyone to hear the good news he had spent his life learning.

## Page 03 - Something changes
Art: books-src/fulton-sheen/art/page-03.webp (present)

In 1930, a new invention was changing the world: radio. Bishop Sheen began a program called The Catholic Hour, and his voice traveled through the air into living rooms across the whole nation.

People who had never entered a church were listening to a priest talk about God while they sat in their own homes. Sheen had found his road.

## Page 04 - A choice becomes action
Art: books-src/fulton-sheen/art/page-04.webp (present)

Then came something even newer: television. In 1952, when television was brand new and nobody knew its rules, Bishop Sheen walked in front of the cameras with only a blackboard and a piece of chalk.

His show was called Life Is Worth Living. Every week, millions of people watched, Catholics and Protestants and everyone else, listening to a bishop in black robes draw on a blackboard and make the Gospel make sense.

## Page 05 - Not everything is easy
Art: books-src/fulton-sheen/art/page-05.webp (present)

It was not easy. Television belonged to comedians and cowboys, not bishops. Sheen had no dancers, no orchestra, no fancy sets, just chalk, a blackboard, stories, humor, and a scholar's mind worn lightly.

Week after week he had to win his audience all over again, against every other show in the country. Week after week, millions chose the bishop with the blackboard.

## Page 06 - Keep going
Art: books-src/fulton-sheen/art/page-06.webp (present)

Sheen kept going for years. In 1966, Pope Paul VI made him Bishop of Rochester, in New York. He served three years, then gave the diocese back and kept preaching and writing almost to the end of his life.

He never stopped learning, and he never stopped sharing. That was the secret of the blackboard: the teacher never became more important than the lesson.

## Page 07 - The story's main moment
Art: books-src/fulton-sheen/art/page-07.webp (present)

In 1953, television gave Bishop Sheen its highest honor, an Emmy Award, for Most Outstanding Personality. A bishop had beaten the biggest stars in America.

Standing there with the trophy, Sheen gave thanks to his writers. His writers, he said, were Matthew, Mark, Luke and John.

Everyone laughed, and everyone understood. The new screen was carrying the old Gospel. The wisdom was never his own, and he never pretended it was.

## Page 08 - What followed
Art: books-src/fulton-sheen/art/page-08.webp (present)

Bishop Sheen preached until the end. He died in December 1979, and he was buried with honor in Saint Patrick's Cathedral in New York. Years later, in 2019, his remains were brought home to Peoria, to the diocese where he had become a priest.

The boy from the Illinois farmland had come home.

## Page 09 - Carry the story home
Art: books-src/fulton-sheen/art/page-09.webp (present)

The Church examined a miracle: the revival of a baby named James Fulton Engstrom, born stillborn in 2010, after his family prayed for Sheen's intercession. The Church recognized it. We report the Church's finding, and we add nothing to it.

On September 24, 2026, in Saint Louis, Missouri, before a crowd of some fifty thousand people, Bishop Sheen was declared Blessed. James himself, now a teenager, carried Sheen's relic in the procession. He is Blessed Fulton Sheen, not Saint yet. The Church waits for one more recognized miracle, and honesty matters more than a title.

The day before, Pope Leo XIV said it best: may his example inspire many others to find new ways of spreading the Gospel with joyful enthusiasm.

## Page 10 - Points of reflection
Art: books-src/fulton-sheen/art/page-10.webp (present)

Bishop Sheen used the newest thing in the world, television, to carry the oldest good news. What do you use every day that could carry something good?

Read together from the Bible: "How can they believe in him if they have never heard about him? And how can they hear about him unless someone tells them?" (Romans 10:14, paraphrased.)

Today, use one modern thing, a message, a call, a drawing, a video, to share something true and good with someone.

Let us pray. Lord, your good news never grows old. Help me share it joyfully, in my own time, in my own way. Blessed Fulton Sheen, pray for us. Amen.

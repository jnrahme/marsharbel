## Plate 1 - Mercatello, 1660 (p01)
Era/place/subjects: 1660 Mercatello, modest stone home interior, green Italian valley through a small window. Newborn Orsola in her mother's arms, father beside, several young sisters around (children present because the scene calls for them).
Composition/lighting: close interior family grouping, warm daylight.
Mandatory negatives: no halo, no heavenly vision, no modern objects; infant not miniature-adult.
Reference basis and limit: text of p01 + New Advent biography (birth Mercatello 1660, parents Francesco and Benedetta); architecture/garments from text-era constraints only, no external image input.
Reader caption/UI metadata only, not pixels: Imaginative devotional illustration.

## Plate 2 - A wish grows in Piacenza (p02)
Era/place/subjects: Piacenza street/doorway, Orsola age 7 sharing bread with two or three poor children.
Composition/lighting: mid exterior, kind daylight, focal on the shared bread.
Mandatory negatives: no beggar caricature, no glamour, no modern street elements; child faces readable and gentle.
Reference basis and limit: "an old story says" framing in p02 - treat as tradition, not documented event; no claim pixels.

## Plate 3 - Her father's other plans (p03)
Era/place/subjects: home interior, father (40s-50s) seated at a wooden table speaking kindly but firmly; adolescent Orsola standing at a window, a distant convent silhouette on a hill outside.
Composition/lighting: interior with window depth, late-afternoon light, emotional tension without conflict.
Mandatory negatives: no argument, no raised hands, no suitors depicted (kept simple); no tears.
Reference basis and limit: p03 text; convent silhouette is symbolic distance, not a documented building.

## Plate 6 - The teacher who prayed (p06)
Era/place/subjects: chapter-room interior, Veronica mid-40s-50s seated teaching four to six novices (new sisters in formation), one open book, attentive faces.

Reference basis and limit: p06 text (34 years novice mistress); Benedict XVI audience for her teaching role.

## Plate 7 - Twenty-two thousand pages (p07)
Era/place/subjects: cell/desk interior, Veronica early 50s writing with a quill by window light, tall neat stacks of handwritten pages, simple wall crucifix.
Composition/lighting: desk close-up, quiet evening light, focus on hand and pages.
Mandatory negatives: NO visible wounds, no blood, no stigmata depiction, no suffering imagery of any kind - the plate shows writing only. No legible text on pages (no letters rule).
Reference basis and limit: p07 text (Diary ordered 1693, ~22,000 pages by hand); "shared in his suffering" is stated in text and deliberately NOT depicted.

## Page 01 - Enter the world
Art: books-src/st-rita/art/page-01.webp (present)

Long ago, about six hundred and fifty years ago, in a tiny village called Roccaporena in the hills of Italy, a baby girl was born. Her name was Margherita, which means pearl, but everyone called her Rita.

Her parents, Antonio and Amata, were famous in their village for something unusual. When families quarreled, people came to them to make peace. Rita grew up watching her mother and father turn enemies back into friends.

## Page 02 - Let us care
Art: books-src/st-rita/art/page-02.webp (present)

Rita learned her faith from the Augustinian friars in nearby Cascia, and she dreamed of becoming a nun. But in those days, parents chose their children's future. Rita's parents arranged a marriage for her, to a man named Paolo, and Rita obeyed.

Rita and Paolo had two sons, and their home was happy. Paolo could be drawn into the angry quarrels of the times, but with Rita's prayer and patience, he slowly learned a more Christian way of life.

## Page 03 - Something changes
Art: books-src/st-rita/art/page-03.webp (present)

Those were violent years. Families carried on feuds, and one day, Paolo was killed by his enemies. Rita's heart broke.

But Rita did something astonishing. She forgave her husband's killers, and she hid Paolo's blood-stained shirt, so that her sons would never see it and be poisoned by revenge. She would not let hatred into her house.

## Page 04 - A choice becomes action
Art: books-src/st-rita/art/page-04.webp (present)

The two families wanted more blood. Rita made prayer her weapon. Day and night she prayed that the feud would stop, that no more blood would be spilled.

Then sorrow came again. Her two sons fell sick and died, one soon after the other. Rita's only comfort was this: their souls were safe with God, far away from the hatred that had taken their father.

## Page 05 - Not everything is easy
Art: books-src/st-rita/art/page-05.webp (present)

Alone in the world, Rita went to the monastery of Saint Mary Magdalene in Cascia and asked to become a nun. The sisters said no. Her family's feud, they feared, could bring violence inside their walls. Rita asked again. Again, no. A third time, no.

Imagine being refused the one thing you want most, three times. Rita did not get angry. She set out to remove the reason for the no.

## Page 06 - Keep going
Art: books-src/st-rita/art/page-06.webp (present)

Rita went herself to the two feuding families. She spoke to them with such courage and gentleness that they agreed to end the feud forever. The families embraced, and they even signed a written peace.

The impossible had happened. The doors of the monastery opened. At thirty-six years old, Rita finally became an Augustinian nun, and she spent the rest of her life in prayer and in caring for the elderly, the sick and the poor.

## Page 07 - The story's main moment
Art: books-src/st-rita/art/page-07.webp (present)

To test Rita's humility, the Abbess gave her a strange job: plant this dry, dead piece of wood, and water it every day. A dead stick. Day after day, Rita watered it without a word of complaint.

And tradition tells us the dead wood grew. It became a living vine, and a vine still grows in that cloister today.

That is Rita's whole story in one picture: with God, dry and dead things bloom.

## Page 08 - What followed
Art: books-src/st-rita/art/page-08.webp (present)

As Rita prayed before a crucifix, she asked to share in Jesus' suffering. A wound like a thorn's mark appeared on her forehead, and it stayed with her for fifteen years. She never boasted of it. She called it only a sore.

In her last winter, ill in bed in the month of January, Rita asked a visiting cousin to bring her a rose and two figs from the garden of her father's house. In the snow, the cousin found them: a rose blooming, and ripe figs. For Rita, it was a sign from God: her husband and her sons were safe in heaven.

One May night, Rita died. Tradition says the monastery bells began to ring by themselves, calling the people of Cascia to her door.

## Page 09 - Carry the story home
Art: books-src/st-rita/art/page-09.webp (present)

People came at once, and miracles were reported in her name. In the year 1900, Pope Leo XIII proclaimed Rita a saint.

People call her the saint of impossible cases. Look at her life and you can see why: forgiveness after murder, a door that opened after three noes, a living vine from a dead stick, and a rose blooming in the snow. With God, nothing was impossible for Rita, and nothing is impossible for you.

## Page 10 - Points of reflection
Art: books-src/st-rita/art/page-10.webp (present)

When something in your life feels impossible, what do you usually do first? What did Rita do?

Read together from the Bible: "Nothing will be impossible for God." (Luke 1:37, paraphrased.)

Today, bring one impossible-feeling thing to God in prayer, and do one small act of peacemaking, at home or at school.

Let us pray. Lord, nothing is impossible for you. When I see only dead wood, give me Rita's patience to keep watering it. Saint Rita, pray for us. Amen.

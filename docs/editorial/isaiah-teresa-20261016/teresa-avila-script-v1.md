# Teresa of Avila: Room for a Friend
English narration draft v1. Ages 6-12, family read-aloud. Review copy, not approved for art, narration or publication. Page headings are not spoken.

## 1. A girl who read about saints
In 1515, in Avila, Spain, Teresa was born into a large family. As she grew, she read stories about saints. Their lives opened a question she would keep returning to: how could she come close to God?

Teresa had brothers, sisters and parents who taught her about faith. She would later write about that childhood, and about discovering that things around us change, while God remains.

But learning to pray was not something she finished as a little girl. It would become the work of her whole life.

## 2. When her mother died
Teresa was about twelve when her mother died. In her account of her life, she remembered turning to Mary and asking her to be her mother.

Years later, Teresa became a pupil of Augustinian nuns in Avila. There, and through spiritual books, she learned about recollection. That means gathering our attention so we can be present to God.

A book could invite her to begin, but prayer asked for something more than reading. It asked her to make room in her own day for God.

## 3. A new name
At twenty, Teresa entered the Carmelite Monastery of the Incarnation in Avila. A monastery is a place where a community shares a life of prayer. She took the name Teresa of Jesus.

Three years later, she became seriously ill. Her illness was long and difficult, and it became part of the life she later described in her writing.

A religious name did not make hardship disappear. Teresa still had to live each day as a person who needed care. Her life of prayer would grow through difficulties, not outside them.

## 4. Beginning again
Teresa did not describe herself as someone who found prayer easy all the time. In her own account, she wrote about struggling to respond to God's call.

She read spiritual books and learned from people who could guide her. She valued the teaching of the Church, and she would keep seeking sound advice throughout her life.

The friendship she later wrote about was not a prize for someone who never struggled. It began with God, who loved her first. Teresa's part was to keep turning toward him.

## 5. Before the image of Jesus
During Lent in 1554, Teresa saw a statue of the wounded Jesus. She was thirty-nine. She later described the deep mark that encounter left on her life.

For Christians, Jesus' suffering speaks of his love for us. Teresa gave more of her attention to him, and her prayer deepened.

This was not a different God from the one she had learned about as a child. It was the same Jesus, becoming central to her life. Her prayer would now lead her into a new and demanding kind of work.

## 6. A house for prayer
In 1562, Teresa founded a reformed Carmelite community in Avila. The city's bishop supported her work. She wanted the sisters' shared life to make room for prayer and service to the Church.

Their way of life called for loving one another, humility and freedom from being ruled by possessions. Humility meant loving the truth, rather than pretending to be more important than someone else.

Teresa was making a practical choice. The friendship with God she wrote about needed time and attention. The sisters would seek that friendship together.

## 7. The work grows
One community was not the end of Teresa's work. In the years that followed, she helped establish more Carmelite convents. Her foundations would number seventeen.

She also worked with John of the Cross. In 1568, their work led to a reformed Carmelite community for men at Duruelo, near Avila.

There were journeys to make, communities to guide, and much to write. Teresa's prayer did not keep her apart from all this work. The woman who taught people to spend time with God also gave her time to serving others.

## 8. The castle inside
In 1577, Teresa wrote a book called The Interior Castle. To describe the life within us, she used the picture of a castle with seven rooms.

She was not telling a story about finding a secret building. The castle was an image that helped people think about growing closer to God. Prayer could deepen through listening, reflection and love.

Teresa also wrote about the Our Father, a prayer many children know. Growing in friendship with Jesus did not mean leaving familiar prayers behind. Even words learned long ago could help someone listen with fresh attention.

## 9. A friendship shared
Teresa died in 1582, in Alba de Tormes, Spain. She had spent years founding communities and writing for people who wanted to pray.

Her life ended, but her books continued to welcome readers. They offered a way to begin where we are, with our own attention, work and need for God.

In 1970, the Church named her a Doctor of the Church. That title recognizes a teacher whose wisdom helps the Church. Teresa's teaching had grown from prayer lived day by day: making room for Jesus, and learning to love other people.

## 10. Points of reflection
What helps you give a friend your attention? Teresa's story invites us to make room for friendship with Jesus too.

With someone who cares for you, read John's Gospel, chapter fifteen, verses twelve to seventeen. Jesus calls his disciples friends and asks them to love one another. How do those two things belong together?

Today, try a short quiet prayer. Tell Jesus something true about your day. Then choose one kind thing to do for someone at home. You can begin again tomorrow.

Let us pray. Jesus, thank you for loving us first. Help us make time to be with you. Teach us to listen, and to share your love in our ordinary days. Amen.

# Isaiah: A Voice for Hope
English narration draft v1. Ages 6-12, family read-aloud. Review copy, not approved for art, narration or publication. Page headings are not spoken.

## 1. A voice in Jerusalem
In the eighth century before Jesus, in Jerusalem, Isaiah received a call that would change his life. Jerusalem was a city in the kingdom of Judah. Its people lived under kings. They had families and neighbors, and difficult choices to make.

The Bible begins Isaiah's calling with a vision in the Temple. Isaiah saw the Lord on a high throne, with the edge of his robe filling the holy place. Before Isaiah could carry a message to anyone else, he encountered the holiness of God.

## 2. More than he could say
In the vision, heavenly beings called seraphim praised God. Their song filled the scene. The doorway shook, and smoke filled the Temple.

Isaiah did not answer with a speech about his own goodness. He spoke about his unclean lips and the unclean lips of his people. Standing before God's holiness, he recognized that he needed forgiveness too.

Then one of the seraphim brought an ember from the altar. In the vision, it touched Isaiah's mouth. This was a sign of cleansing, preparing him for what came next.

## 3. Here I am
Isaiah heard the Lord asking who would carry his message. Isaiah answered that he was ready to be sent.

He was being sent to his own people. The task would not be easy. They would not always listen, and Isaiah's message included warnings about the harm their choices could bring.

His answer did not make him a king or give him an army. He became a prophet: someone called to speak God's message. The man who had confessed his unclean lips would now use his voice in God's service.

## 4. The neighbor who needs help
What did that message ask of people? They were to stop doing wrong and learn to do good. They were to seek justice, defend people who had been wronged, and listen to those without someone to protect them.

Isaiah spoke of orphans and widows, people who could be pushed aside. Prayer and worship could not be separated from their treatment of a neighbor.

His message reached beyond the Temple. It belonged wherever someone was ignored, treated unfairly, or left without help. Faith had something to say about those places too.

## 5. A frightened king
During Isaiah's ministry, two neighboring kings threatened Judah. The Bible says King Ahaz and his people trembled like trees in the wind.

God sent Isaiah to meet Ahaz, taking his son with him. Their meeting place was beside a water channel outside the city. Isaiah carried a message urging the king to remain calm and trust God.

But Ahaz chose to depend on the power of Assyria. Isaiah had spoken; the king still had a choice. Answering God's call did not mean that everyone would accept the answer.

## 6. A name to remember
In that time of danger, Isaiah also spoke of a child called Emmanuel. The name means "with us is God."

The sign first spoke to the fears of Ahaz and his people. Many centuries later, Christians would hear these words in the story of Jesus' birth. In Jesus, God truly came to live among us.

Isaiah's book holds warnings, but it also holds hope. A frightened king was not the end of the story. The promises of God reached further than the danger people could see before them.

## 7. Outside the walls
Years later, another king, Hezekiah, ruled Judah. Once again, the kingdom faced a powerful enemy. In 701 before Jesus, Assyria threatened Jerusalem itself.

Isaiah continued to call his people to trust God rather than rely only on political plans. The Bible tells how Jerusalem was delivered from that attack, as Isaiah had promised.

This was a particular event in the people's history. It does not mean that faithful people never face loss. Isaiah's work took place in a troubled world, where courage and trust were needed again and again.

## 8. A little green shoot
There is another picture of hope in Isaiah's book: a fresh shoot growing from an old stump. A tree that looks finished still has life in its roots.

The passage describes a ruler guided by God's Spirit, who judges fairly and cares for the poor. It pictures peace through surprising neighbors: a wolf beside a lamb, and a little child leading them.

These are prophetic pictures, not instructions to approach wild animals. Christians see in this promised ruler the hope fulfilled in Jesus. Power can serve justice. What looks broken need not have the last word.

## 9. The voice carries on
We know little about Isaiah's final days. But the message did not end when his own voice fell silent.

The Book of Isaiah gathers his preaching and the work of later disciples. Some lived long after him, carrying forward its call and its hope. These different voices became part of the book Christians still read today.

In churches and homes, Isaiah's answer is heard again. It began with a man who needed forgiveness, and who was willing to be sent. His voice still invites us to listen for God.

## 10. Points of reflection
What made Isaiah's answer brave? He agreed to speak before knowing whether people would listen. You can talk about that with someone who cares for you.

Read Isaiah chapter six, verse eight, together. Then read chapter one, verses sixteen and seventeen. Notice how answering God connects with doing good for other people.

Today, choose one small way to listen or help. You might include someone in a game, or tell a trusted grown-up when someone is being treated unfairly.

Let us pray. God of hope, help us listen to you. Give us courage to tell the truth and kindness to notice our neighbors. Show us one good thing we can do today. Amen.

Dafne Gutierrez: The Phoenix Blindness Healing

In January 2016, a legally blind mother of four in Phoenix, Arizona, prayed before the relics of Saint Charbel, went to confession, and was anointed with blessed oil. Within days her vision measured 20/20 and her optic nerves read normal. A medical committee reviewing her records concluded it had no medical explanation.

The Person Before

Dafne Gutierrez was a Catholic mother of four in Phoenix, Arizona. She suffered from benign intracranial hypertension - increased pressure inside the skull - which damages the optic nerves by constriction. In 2012 the condition cost her the vision in her right eye; in November 2015, her left eye failed as well. She was legally blind, and beginning to suffer seizures. For more than a year she learned to live without sight - and, she feared, without ever seeing her children again.

What Happened

In January 2016, Saint Charbel's relics came to St Joseph Maronite Church in Phoenix as part of their American tour. Gutierrez was not a Maronite Catholic - her sister encouraged her to go. On January 16 she prayed before the relics, went to confession, and was anointed with blessed oil by the pastor, Father Wissam Akiki. She recalled that immediately afterward her body felt "different."

The sequence that followed is documented in her medical record. The next morning, still impaired, she called her ophthalmologist; an exam the following day showed her still legally blind, with abnormal optic nerves. Two days later a second ophthalmologist measured her vision at a perfect 20/20 - with completely normal optic nerves. A week later, her original ophthalmologist confirmed it: normal vision, normal exam. Her seizures stopped as well.

The Medical Evidence

Dr. Anne Borik, a board-certified internal medicine physician, was called in by the Church to review the case and led a medical committee through her records and repeated examinations. Before-and-after images told the story: "We have pictures to confirm that the optic disc was chronically atrophied," she told the National Catholic Register. "In the post-healing pictures, her optic disc is back to normal... That is why I, as a medical doctor, have no explanation." The committee's written conclusion: "After a thorough physical exam, extensive literature search and review of all medical records, we have no medical explanation and therefore believe this to be a miraculous healing through the intercession of St. Charbel."

How the Church Treated It

The case was examined at the level of the Maronite Eparchy: the medical committee's review was commissioned by the Church, and Bishop Abdallah Elias Zaidan publicly attributed the healing to Saint Charbel's intercession, writing that he hoped it would inspire the faithful "to seek the spiritual sight." That is a bishop's recognition, not a Vatican declaration - the case was not sent to Rome as part of a canonization cause. We present it at exactly that level: a medically reviewed healing, acknowledged by her Church, reported by the National Catholic Register.

What It Means for Pilgrims Today

Dafne Gutierrez never traveled to Lebanon. Her encounter happened because the relics traveled - one parish stop among dozens on the American tour. For pilgrims who cannot reach Annaya, her case is the standing reminder that the devotion lives in parishes: a relic visit, a confession, the oil, and honest prayer. And the discipline of her story is the discipline of this site: her healing began and ended in doctors' offices, and prayer accompanied medicine rather than replacing it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is Dafne Gutierrez?

A mother of four from Phoenix, Arizona. Legally blind from benign intracranial hypertension since November 2015, she reported her sight restored in January 2016 after praying before Saint Charbel's relics - a recovery her reviewing physicians could not explain.

What did the doctors say about Dafne Gutierrez?

A medical committee led by Dr. Anne Borik reviewed her records and examinations and wrote that they had no medical explanation, believing it 'a miraculous healing through the intercession of St. Charbel.' Before-and-after imaging showed her atrophied optic disc returned to normal.

Is the Dafne Gutierrez healing Vatican-approved?

No. It was reviewed at the eparchial (diocesan) level and publicly acknowledged by Bishop Zaidan of the Maronite Eparchy. It was not submitted to Rome as part of a canonization cause.

Where exactly was Dafne Gutierrez healed?

At St Joseph Maronite Church in Phoenix, Arizona, on January 16, 2016, during the American tour of Saint Charbel's relics - after prayer before the relics, confession, and anointing with blessed oil.

What is benign intracranial hypertension?

A condition of increased pressure inside the skull, which can constrict and permanently damage the optic nerves. It cost Dafne Gutierrez the sight of one eye in 2012 and the other in 2015 - the damage her physicians later documented as reversed.

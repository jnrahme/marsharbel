The ancient witness · Lanciano, Italy · Eighth century, traditionally dated

Lanciano: A Witness Kept in Abruzzo

An ancient account of doubt at Mass, and a presence still venerated in a Franciscan sanctuary.

01

The story as the sanctuary tells it

A monk of the Eastern rite was celebrating Mass in Lanciano when he struggled with belief in the Real Presence. According to the sanctuary tradition, the bread and wine visibly changed. The monk is unnamed. The sanctuary places the account broadly in the eighth century, perhaps between 730 and 750; it says no source fixes an exact day or year.

02

The witness today

The relics remain at the Church of San Francesco. The sanctuary traces their preservation through successive communities and describes examinations over the centuries. A written account dating to 1631 is much later than the event it describes, a distinction worth keeping in view.

03

What examination can say

The sanctuary reports laboratory examination of samples in 1970-71, including findings described as human cardiac tissue and blood group AB. Those are its account of a particular examination; a laboratory result cannot by itself establish the ancient event, divine causation, or a complete chain of custody across twelve centuries.

Pause and reflect

In the story, doubt is not the end of prayer. Bring difficult questions into the quiet of the Mass without demanding that a sign replace faith.

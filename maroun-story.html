Saint Maroun: The Hermit on the Hill

Climb a Syrian hill, meet the hermit who prayed under the open sky, and see how a whole family of faith grew from one quiet life. Read together, then listen.

Short answer: This ten-page Saint Maroun storybook for ages 6 to 12 follows a hermit of the late 300s from his hilltop near Cyrrhus to the Maronite family that carries his name. It ends with a narrated family reflection to share together.

Story Summary

Saint Maroun lived in the open air on a hilltop in northern Syria. People climbed up to meet him, and his way of prayer spread. Nine narrative pages end with a narrated family reflection.

    About this story

    Illustrations are imaginative composites, not portraits or eyewitness scenes. Maroun's life is known mostly from one chapter by Theodoret of Cyrrhus, who never met him, so the story leaves out his childhood and family because nobody knows them. The letter from John Chrysostom is addressed to a monk named Maron, and many people believe he meant this Maroun. The Maronite Church traces its name to him, though its early history is still debated by historians.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Who was Saint Maroun?

    A hermit of the late 300s who lived on a hilltop near Cyrrhus in northern Syria. The Maronite Church is named after him. His feast is on 9 February.

    Is the story true?

    It follows the bishop Theodoret's chapter about Maroun, the one close account of his life. Where the sources are silent, the story says so and invents nothing.

    Who is this storybook for?

    Children ages 6 to 12, reading or listening with family. Ten illustrated pages end with a four-part family reflection.

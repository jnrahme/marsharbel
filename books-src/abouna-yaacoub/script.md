# Blessed Abouna Yaacoub — children's storybook script (v1 draft)
# Ages 6-12 read-aloud. Ten pages. Promise: "One man walked his whole country to share good news, then spent the rest of his life building places where forgotten people were loved."
# Thread: the walking feet, then the Cross on the hill.

## Page 1 — Enter the world
On February 1, 1875, in Ghazir, a mountain village in Lebanon, a boy named Jacques Ghazir Haddad was born. He was the third of five children, and his home sat in green hills high above the sea.

Jacques studied hard. He learned Arabic, French and Syriac at a school in Beirut, the big city down by the water.

## Page 2 — Let us care
When Jacques was seventeen, he traveled far away to Egypt, to the city of Alexandria, to teach Arabic. He was good at his work. But far from home, he felt something new: a call to give his life to God as a priest.

The next year he came back to his own hills and joined the Capuchin friars at the convent of Khashbau.

## Page 3 — Something changes
On November 1, 1901, Jacques was ordained a priest in Beirut. The people of Lebanon did not call him Father Jacques. They called him Abouna Yaacoub - Father Jacob.

Abouna means "our father" in Arabic. It is what Lebanese people lovingly call their priests. Jacques had become a father to everyone.

## Page 4 — A choice becomes action
Abouna Yaacoub looked at his country and made a choice. He put a knapsack on his shoulder and started walking. From 1903 to 1914, for eleven years, he walked Lebanon on foot, preaching in village after village.

He walked so far, and told so many people about Jesus, that he earned a name that stayed with him forever: the Apostle of Lebanon. He even preached beyond his country, in Syria, Palestine, Iraq and Turkey.

## Page 5 — Not everything is easy
Walking was only the beginning. Everywhere Abouna Yaacoub went, he saw people nobody was helping: the sick, orphans, beggars, girls with no home. Preaching was not enough for them. They needed places to be cared for.

Where do you even start, when the need is so big? Abouna Yaacoub started with one hill.

## Page 6 — Keep going
In 1919 he bought a piece of land on the hill of Jal el Dib, north of Beirut. He built a small chapel to Our Lady of the Sea, and beside it he raised a great Cross that everyone could see.

Then he founded the Franciscan Sisters of the Holy Cross of Lebanon, sisters who would care for the people arriving at the Cross. And the works kept growing: a home for orphan girls, a hospital for the old and sick, another hospital, a home for beggars, a home for girls with nowhere to go. And schools: one, then ten, then, according to his Capuchin order, two hundred and thirty schools for seven and a half thousand students.

## Page 7 — The story's main moment
The great Cross on the hill became the sign of everything Abouna Yaacoub did. His sisters were Sisters of the Cross. His works were called the works of the Cross. His whole life gathered at the foot of that Cross.

Why a Cross? Because the Cross is where Jesus loved the people everyone else forgot. Abouna Yaacoub spent his life loving exactly those people, in exactly that way.

## Page 8 — What followed
Abouna Yaacoub spent himself without holding anything back. Worn by vigils, travel and illness, nearly blind, and finally sick with leukemia, he stayed awake and clear to the end. His last hours were one unbroken chain of prayers to the Cross and the Virgin Mary.

He died on June 26, 1954. Lebanon mourned its walking father.

## Page 9 — Carry the story home
The Church investigated a miracle attributed to Abouna Yaacoub's prayers, and Pope Benedict XVI approved his beatification. We report the Church's finding, and we add nothing to it.

On June 22, 2008, in Beirut, in the very country he had walked end to end, he was declared Blessed. The boy from Ghazir who once taught far from home had become a father to a whole nation.

## Page 10 — Points of reflection
Abouna Yaacoub carried good news with his feet for eleven years. What good news could you carry to someone this week?

Read together from the Bible: "How beautiful on the mountains are the feet of those who bring good news." (Isaiah 52:7, paraphrased.)

Today, carry one piece of good news or one kind word to someone, at home, at school, or in your neighborhood.

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you walked among us to bring good news. Help my feet and my words carry your love to others. Blessed Abouna Yaacoub, pray for us. Amen.

# Blessed Estephan Nehme — children's storybook script (v1 draft)
# Ages 6-12 read-aloud. Ten pages. Promise: "Through a hidden life of work and prayer, Brother Estephan shows us that God sees every small thing we do."
# Refrain (sourced as his constant saying): "God sees me."

## Page 1 — Enter the world
In March 1889, in Lehfed, a mountain village in Lebanon, a boy named Youssef was born. He was the youngest of seven children in a family that prayed together and worked their fields together.

An old village tale tells about the day Youssef was watching his father's animals. A badger slipped into a cave. Youssef dug where it vanished, and water rose from the ground. People still call that spring the Badger's Fountain. The boy who found hidden water would spend his whole life on hidden goodness.

## Page 2 — Let us care
Youssef went to a small school run by monks, and he grew up working his father's fields. He learned early that work and prayer belong together.

When Youssef was fourteen, his father died. Two years later, in 1905, he left home for a monastery at Kfifan, in the hills of North Lebanon.

## Page 3 — Something changes
At the monastery of Saints Cyprian and Justina, Youssef became a novice. A novice is a beginner in monastic life, learning the way of the monks. On August 23, 1907, he made his first vows. He took a new name: Estephan, after the patron saint of his home village.

A new name means a new life. Estephan was giving his whole life to God.

## Page 4 — A choice becomes action
Brother Estephan never became a priest. He stayed a lay brother, a monk who serves God through work instead of leading prayer at the altar. He ploughed fields, planted gardens, and built with wood and stone.

He had his own name for the land he worked. He called it "a school of sanctity." Every furrow could teach him something about being good.

## Page 5 — Not everything is easy
When Estephan was a young brother, a terrible war came, and with it a famine. Families in the mountains had no food. Estephan carried food from the monastery to families who were starving.

The brothers who worked beside him remembered his generosity, and his quiet care for anyone in difficulty.

## Page 6 — Keep going
For thirty-one years, Brother Estephan moved between monasteries: Mayfouk, Houb, Kettara, Jbeil. Wherever he went, he worked hard and prayed deeply.

Then something unusual happened. Other monasteries began to ask for him by name. Everyone wanted the brother who worked, and the brother who prayed.

## Page 7 — The story's main moment
The brothers who lived beside Estephan heard him say one sentence again and again: "God sees me."

He did not say it to be afraid. He said it because he was never alone. Every hour in the field, every plank of wood, every silence was lived in front of Someone who loved him.

Three small words held his whole way of life.

## Page 8 — What followed
In the summer of 1938, Estephan became very ill with a fever. He died at Kfifan on August 30, 1938. He was forty-nine years old, and the brothers buried him in the monastery cemetery.

He had been so hidden that almost nothing about him was written down. But years later, in 1951, his tomb was opened, and his body was found whole and intact. For the faithful, this was a seal on a life of hidden purity. Pilgrims began to come.

## Page 9 — Carry the story home
The Church looked carefully at Estephan's life, and at a healing reported through his prayers. In 2010, Pope Benedict XVI recognized a miracle: the healing of a Maronite sister, Marina Nehme, who had prayed to her uncle for years. We report the Church's finding, and we add nothing to it.

On June 27, 2010, crowds gathered at Kfifan as Estephan was declared Blessed. The brother whom almost nobody wrote about is now honored by the whole Church. The boy who found a hidden spring had been right all along. God saw him.

## Page 10 — Points of reflection
Brother Estephan said "God sees me" while he dug, planted and built. What made that sentence a comfort to him instead of a worry?

Read together from the Bible: "Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord." (Colossians 3:23, paraphrased.)

Today, choose one small job: setting the table, finishing homework, helping a neighbor. Do it carefully, as if for God.

Let us pray. Lord, you see every small thing I do. Help me to do today's work with love. Blessed Estephan, pray for us. Amen.

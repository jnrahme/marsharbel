    Read the Bible and pray together

    With a parent or caregiver, read John 20:11-18. Listen for the moment when Mary recognizes Jesus and the message he gives her.

    The final page offers a question, Bible reading, a listening action and an original prayer. A trusted adult can help children with the sad parts of the story. Prayer does not replace rest, care or treatment.

    About this story

    Illustrations are imaginative composites, not eyewitness reconstructions. This story uses Luke for Mary's earlier discipleship and John for the Easter encounter; the large stone is a detail from Matthew. It does not identify Mary Magdalene with Mary of Bethany or the unnamed woman who anointed Jesus. Page eight shows later Christian remembrance, not Mary alive in a later century. The final reflection is original, not historical dialogue.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Who was Mary Magdalene?

    A disciple of Jesus who supported his ministry and witnessed his resurrection. This retelling follows Luke's account of her discipleship and John's account of the Easter encounter.

    Who is this storybook for?

    Children ages 6 to 12, reading or listening with family. Nine illustrated narrative pages lead to a four-part family reflection.

    Why is she called the apostle of the apostles?

    The title honors the resurrection message Mary brought to the disciples. It does not mean that she was one of the Twelve. The Church celebrates her feast on July 22.

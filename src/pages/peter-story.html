The Story of Saint Peter

A fisherman follows Jesus, learns to trust, makes a painful mistake, and discovers that forgiveness can give him a new beginning.

Short answer: This Saint Peter storybook for ages 6 to 12 follows Simon Peter from his fishing boat to life as an apostle. Scripture records his call, his denial, the risen Jesus forgiving him, Pentecost and his release from prison. Ancient tradition describes his death in Rome. It ends with four points of reflection.

    Read the Bible and pray together

    With a trusted adult, read Luke 5:1-11. Jesus meets Simon Peter by the lake and invites him to follow. Ask together: what is one ordinary thing we can do with love today?

    Pray: "Jesus, help me stay close to you, tell the truth, and begin again when I make a mistake. Amen." This is an original family prayer, not a liturgical text.

    Choose one kind act this week: help at home, include someone who feels left out, or tell a trusted adult about a mistake and ask how to put it right.

    About this story

    These scenes are imaginative illustrations, not historical footage. The modern child and grandparent beside Saint Peter's Basilica on pages nine and ten are reflection scenes, not Peter's burial or martyrdom. Scripture events, ancient tradition and the author's reflections are distinguished in the story. The final narrated page offers four points of reflection.

    Sources: Benedict XVI: Peter the fisherman; Benedict XVI: Peter the apostle; Acts 2; Acts 12; Eusebius: Church History; Jerome: On Illustrious Men, chapter 1; Saint Peter’s Necropolis.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Who was Saint Peter?

    A fisherman from Galilee called by Jesus to become an apostle. Scripture tells of his trust, his failure and forgiveness, and his leadership after Pentecost.

    Who is this storybook for?

    Children ages 6 to 12, reading or listening with family. The final narrated page includes four points of reflection.

    Which parts come from Scripture?

    The call, denial, forgiveness, Pentecost and escape from prison follow Gospel and Acts accounts. Peter’s death in Rome follows ancient Christian tradition. Reflections and illustrations are authored retellings, not eyewitness records.

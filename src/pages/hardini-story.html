    Youssef Kassab was born in 1808 in the village of Hardin in North Lebanon. In 1828 he entered the Lebanese Maronite Order and took the name Nimatullah, which means "grace of God" in Arabic. He was ordained a priest on December 25, 1833.

    At the monastery of Kfifan he directed the young monks' studies and taught theology. One of his students was Charbel Makhlouf. He also founded free schools for the children of the mountain villages. Pope John Paul II called him "a man of prayer, enamored of the Eucharist." He died on December 14, 1858.

    Key teaching

    Nimatullah joined prayer and teaching. He was devoted to the Eucharist, and he spent his life teaching young monks and the children of poor villages.

    These scenes are imaginative illustrations, not historical footage. The hillside village, bookbinding hands, and classroom are composites, not a record of one afternoon.

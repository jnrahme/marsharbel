A Book for Ages 6-12

Saint Marina: The Girl Who Became Brother Marinos

Walk into the Qadisha Valley with a girl who stayed beside her father, was blamed for something she did not do, and kept faith. Read together, then listen.

Short answer: This ten-page Saint Marina storybook for ages 6 to 12 retells the Lebanese tradition of Marina the Monk, from her father's calling to the monastery to the day the truth was learned. It ends with a narrated family reflection to share together.

Story Summary

Saint Marina is remembered by Maronites in the Qadisha Valley of Lebanon. Her story is a tradition passed on for a very long time. Nine narrative pages end with a narrated family reflection.

    About this story

    Illustrations are imaginative composites, not portraits or eyewitness scenes. Marina's story comes from Lebanese tradition, written down in later sources. Historians do not agree on when she lived, and the Orthodox Church tells a different version of the story, so this book gives no dates. The details about the milk and the monk who was blind in one eye are told as 'the old story says'. The last two pages mention modern times: a pilgrimage in 2018 and the chapel visitors can see today.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Who was Saint Marina?

    A woman remembered in Lebanese tradition as Marina the Monk, who lived as Brother Marinos at the Monastery of Qannoubine in the Qadisha Valley.

    Is the story true?

    It is a tradition recorded in later sources, so the book gives no dates and tells uncertain details as 'the old story says'.

    Who is this storybook for?

    Children ages 6 to 12, reading or listening with family. Ten illustrated pages end with a four-part family reflection.

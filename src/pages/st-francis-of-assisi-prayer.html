Prayers of the Saints - Feast October 4

Canticle of the Sun: the Prayer of St. Francis

The prayer people most often attach to Saint Francis, “Lord, make me an instrument of your peace,” is not his. The prayer that is his is the Canticle of the Sun, a song of praise he made near the end of his life, while ill, in a hut beside San Damiano. This page gives its words as translated in 1906, with nothing changed.

The Canticle of the Sun

Also called the Canticle of the Creatures or the Praises of the Creatures. The translation below is Paschal Robinson’s of 1906 (public domain), made from the oldest Assisi manuscript. It aims at the plain sense of the original, not at rhyme.

The Canticle of the Sun

Most high, omnipotent, good Lord,
Praise, glory and honor and benediction all, are Thine.
To Thee alone do they belong, most High,
And there is no man fit to mention Thee.

Praise be to Thee, my Lord, with all Thy creatures,
Especially to my worshipful brother sun,
The which lights up the day, and through him dost Thou brightness give;
And beautiful is he and radiant with splendor great;
Of Thee, most High, signification gives.

Praised be my Lord, for sister moon and for the stars,
In heaven Thou hast formed them clear and precious and fair.

Praised be my Lord for brother wind
And for the air and clouds and fair and every kind of weather,
By the which Thou givest to Thy creatures nourishment.

Praised be my Lord for sister water,
The which is greatly helpful and humble and precious and pure.

Praised be my Lord for brother fire,
By the which Thou lightest up the dark.
And fair is he and gay and mighty and strong.

Praised be my Lord for our sister, mother earth,
The which sustains and keeps us
And brings forth diverse fruits with grass and flowers bright.

Praised be my Lord for those who for Thy love forgive
And weakness bear and tribulation.
Blessed those who shall in peace endure,
For by Thee, most High, shall they be crowned.

Praised be my Lord for our sister, the bodily death,
From the which no living man can flee.
Woe to them who die in mortal sin;
Blessed those who shall find themselves in Thy most holy will,
For the second death shall do them no ill.

Praise ye and bless ye my Lord, and give Him thanks,
And be subject unto Him with great humility.

Purpose
Praise. Every verse thanks God for a creature, and the last verses praise Him for forgiveness and for death. Nothing in it is a request.
How to pray
Aloud and slowly, ideally outdoors or at a window. Pause after each verse and look at what it names: the sun, the moon, wind, water, fire, earth.
When & how long
Morning is the natural hour. About three minutes. It is also a fitting prayer for his feast, October 4.
Source & tradition
Written by Saint Francis in the Umbrian dialect of Italian, late in 1225, at San Damiano. Tradition, from the Mirror of Perfection, says he added the verses on forgiveness and on death later, in the last year of his life. Accepted as authentic by the scholars Boehmer and Goetz.
Spiritual fruit
Gratitude, and the habit of seeing creation as a gift from God and not as a resource. It is a prayer of praise, not of petition, and it makes no promises.

How to Pray with Saint Francis

1. Pray it as praise

Francis made it while ill and in pain. It asks for nothing. Let it be the one prayer of your day that is only thanks.

2. Pray it outside

Go to a window, a garden, or a doorway if you can. The prayer names things you can see.

3. Take a verse a day

The Canticle is short, but one verse a morning for nine days is a gentle way to learn it by heart.

4. Keep the last verses

The verses on forgiveness and on death were, by tradition, his last additions. They are the hardest and the most honest part of the prayer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the prayer of Saint Francis?

The Canticle of the Sun is the prayer we can be sure he wrote. The best-known “Peace Prayer” first appeared in print in France in 1912 and was later attributed to him; it is a beautiful prayer, but it is not from his pen. See the note on his main page.

What does the Canticle of the Sun mean?

It praises God through the things He made, and calls them brother and sister. It does not worship nature: every verse is addressed to the Lord, and the creatures are the reason for praise.

Why does it call death a sister?

Francis wrote the last verse as he was dying. He treats death as part of creation that God also made, and blesses those who meet it in God’s will.

Which translation is this?

Paschal Robinson’s 1906 English version, printed with an imprimatur. He says it is a literal rendering of the Assisi manuscript, so its rhythm is plainer than a rhymed version.

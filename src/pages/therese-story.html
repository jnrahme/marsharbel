    Therese Martin was born in Alencon, France on January 2, 1873. Her mother died when she was four, and the family moved to Lisieux. At 14, on a pilgrimage to Rome, she asked Pope Leo XIII for permission to enter the Carmelite convent at fifteen. She entered the Carmel of Lisieux on April 9, 1888.

    In Carmel she wrote that love is at the center of everything, and she called her path the "little way" of trust and everyday love. She wrote her life story at the request of her superiors. She died of tuberculosis on September 30, 1897, at 24, and her last words were "My God, I love you!" She was canonized in 1925, and Pope John Paul II named her a Doctor of the Church on October 19, 1997.

    About this story

    Illustrations are original imaginative composites, not historical footage or records of private conversations. Pages nine and ten show modern families reflecting on Therese, not scenes from her life. Ordinary roses are a motif, not a promised miraculous sign.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Who was Saint Therese of Lisieux?

    Therese Martin was born in Alencon, France in 1873. She entered Carmel in Lisieux at fifteen and taught the little way of trust and daily love.

    Who is this storybook for?

    Children ages 6 to 12, reading or listening with a trusted adult. The final page has a question, Bible reading, safe action and prayer.

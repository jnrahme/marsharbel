A Book for Ages 5-12

The Story of Mother Teresa

A true story told in simple words: a girl from Skopje who taught in India, learned to care for neighbors who were ill, and welcomed others into a life of prayer and service.

Short answer: This read-aloud Mother Teresa storybook for children ages 5 to 12 tells how Gonxha of Skopje became a teacher in India, heard what she called a call within a call on a train to Darjeeling in 1946, and went to care for the poorest of the poor in Calcutta. It also tells of the sisters who joined her.

Story Summary

This read-aloud storybook tells children ages 5 to 12 the true story of Mother Teresa: the girl Gonxha from Skopje who became a teacher in India, heard what she called a call within a call on a train to Darjeeling in 1946, and went into the slums of Calcutta to care for the poorest of the poor. It tells of the sisters who joined her, the work that grew around the world, and the long hidden darkness she carried in prayer.

    About this story

    These scenes are imaginative illustrations, not historical footage. The train experience is Teresa's reported interior call, not a voice heard by fellow passengers. Some ordinary scenes are composites, not recorded afternoons. The final page is a narrated "Points of reflection" invitation to put neighbor-love into practice.

    Sources: Holy See biography, Missionaries of Charity, Nobel Prize, and 2016 canonization homily. Read more on the Mother Teresa biography page.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Who was Mother Teresa?

    Born Agnes Gonxhe Bojaxhiu in Skopje in 1910, she founded the Missionaries of Charity in Calcutta, received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979, and was canonized on September 4, 2016.

    Who is this storybook for?

    Children ages 5 to 12, reading on their own or listening with family. Every page has a Read Aloud button with gentle narration, so the book can be heard as well as read.

    Is the story true?

    Yes. It retells documented events. The book's own notes name the reported interior experiences, like the call on the train, as reported experiences, and explain that some ordinary scenes are composites.

    Where can I read more about Mother Teresa?

    The St Teresa of Calcutta page on this site tells her full story for adults, with sources and her own words.

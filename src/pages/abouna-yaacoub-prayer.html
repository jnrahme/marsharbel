Blessed of Lebanon - Memorial June 26

Praying with Blessed Abouna Yaacoub

Blessed Abouna Yaacoub Haddad was a Lebanese Capuchin, a friar of the order of Friars Minor Capuchin, and the founder of the Franciscan Sisters of the Holy Cross. No prayer of his own is given in the sources used for this site, and this page does not invent one. What it offers is the Canticle of the Sun, the best-known prayer of Saint Francis, which belongs to the Franciscan family he lived in. It is a fitting choice, not his words, and the sources do not say he prayed it.

The Canticle of the Sun, a Franciscan Prayer

This is the same text as on the page for Saint Francis: the translation by Paschal Robinson of 1906 (public domain), made from the oldest Assisi manuscript. It is printed as published. It is the prayer of Saint Francis and is offered here as a fitting prayer for a Capuchin friar and for the sisters he founded.

The Canticle of the Sun

Most high, omnipotent, good Lord,
Praise, glory and honor and benediction all, are Thine.
To Thee alone do they belong, most High,
And there is no man fit to mention Thee.

Praise be to Thee, my Lord, with all Thy creatures,
Especially to my worshipful brother sun,
The which lights up the day, and through him dost Thou brightness give;
And beautiful is he and radiant with splendor great;
Of Thee, most High, signification gives.

Praised be my Lord, for sister moon and for the stars,
In heaven Thou hast formed them clear and precious and fair.

Praised be my Lord for brother wind
And for the air and clouds and fair and every kind of weather,
By the which Thou givest to Thy creatures nourishment.

Praised be my Lord for sister water,
The which is greatly helpful and humble and precious and pure.

Praised be my Lord for brother fire,
By the which Thou lightest up the dark.
And fair is he and gay and mighty and strong.

Praised be my Lord for our sister, mother earth,
The which sustains and keeps us
And brings forth diverse fruits with grass and flowers bright.

Praised be my Lord for those who for Thy love forgive
And weakness bear and tribulation.
Blessed those who shall in peace endure,
For by Thee, most High, shall they be crowned.

Praised be my Lord for our sister, the bodily death,
From the which no living man can flee.
Woe to them who die in mortal sin;
Blessed those who shall find themselves in Thy most holy will,
For the second death shall do them no ill.

Praise ye and bless ye my Lord, and give Him thanks,
And be subject unto Him with great humility.

Purpose
Praise. Every verse thanks God for a creature, and the last verses praise Him for forgiveness and for death. Nothing in it is a request.
How to pray
Aloud and slowly, ideally outdoors or at a window. Pause after each verse and look at what it names. You may offer it in thanks for the works of mercy that grew from the Cross at Jal el Dib.
When & how long
Morning is the natural hour. About three minutes. His memorial is June 26.
Source & tradition
Written by Saint Francis of Assisi in the Umbrian dialect of Italian, late in 1225; English translation by Paschal Robinson, 1906, public domain. Labeled a fitting Franciscan prayer. It is not a prayer of Abouna Yaacoub, and no source used here records that he prayed it. See the Francis page for the full note on its origin.
Spiritual fruit
Gratitude, and seeing creation as a gift from God. It is a prayer of praise, not of petition, and it makes no promises.

How to Pray with Blessed Abouna Yaacoub

1. Read his page first

His page covers the walking preacher, the Cross at Jal el Dib, and the hospitals, schools and orphanages he founded.

2. Pray it as praise

The Canticle asks for nothing. Let it be the one prayer of your day that is only thanks.

3. Remember the family

His order, the Friars Minor Capuchin, and the Franciscan Sisters of the Holy Cross he founded both belong to the Franciscan tradition that Francis began. That is why this prayer is a fitting one.

4. Turn it into service

His life joined prayer to care for the poor and the sick. End by naming one act of care you can do this week.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did Abouna Yaacoub write a prayer?

Nothing in the sources used on this site records one. This page does not invent one.

Why the Canticle of the Sun?

He was a Capuchin, a friar of the Franciscan family, and he founded the Franciscan Sisters of the Holy Cross. The Canticle is the best-known prayer of Saint Francis. It is a fitting choice, not a record that he prayed it.

Who wrote the Canticle?

Saint Francis of Assisi, about 1225. The English here is Paschal Robinson’s 1906 translation, in the public domain.

Is he a saint?

He is Blessed: he was beatified in Beirut on June 22, 2008. His page explains this.

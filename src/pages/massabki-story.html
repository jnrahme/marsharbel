    Francis, Abdel Moati and Raphael Massabki were Maronite laymen of Damascus. Francis was a silk merchant and father of eight. Abdel Moati was a schoolteacher and father of five. Raphael, the youngest, was unmarried and helped in the family shop and the parish church.

    On the night of July 9-10, 1860, violence against Christians reached Damascus. The brothers took shelter in the Franciscan convent of Saint Paul. They were offered their lives if they would give up their faith, and they refused. Eight Franciscan friars were killed with them. The Church honors all eleven as the Martyrs of Damascus. They were beatified in 1926 and canonized in 2024, the first Maronite laymen to be canonized.

    Key teaching

    The Massabki brothers were laymen: a merchant, a teacher and a young man from the parish. They show that holiness can mean staying faithful to Christ in ordinary work, even when it costs everything.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Who were the Massabki brothers?

    Francis, Mooti, and Raphael were three Maronite Christian laymen in Damascus, canonized with eight Franciscan friars in 2024.

    Who is this storybook for?

    Children ages 6 to 12 with a trusted adult. Pause and talk together if the sad part feels frightening.

    Does this book show violence?

    No. It tells the truth about their deaths without depicting violence. It teaches children to seek safety and adult help, and never blame a whole community for the acts of attackers.

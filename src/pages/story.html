A Book for Ages 5-12

The Gentle Light of Saint Charbel

A true story told in simple words: a boy from Lebanon who loved Jesus deeply, became a monk, lived in silence, and inspired hope and healing for people from many places and backgrounds.

Short answer: This is a read-aloud Saint Charbel storybook for children ages 5 to 12, told in simple words. It follows the boy from Bekaa Kafra who became a monk in 1851, a priest in 1859 and the hermit of Annaya, and closes with his death in 1898 and his canonization in 1977.

Story Summary

This read-aloud storybook tells the true story of Saint Charbel Makhlouf for children ages 5 to 12. It follows the boy from Bekaa Kafra who lost his father young, chose a life of silence and prayer, became a monk in 1851 and a priest in 1859, and lived his last twenty-three years as the hermit of Annaya. It closes with his death in 1898 and his canonization in 1977, and it shows, in simple words, why people around the world still ask for his prayers.

Evidence for This Page

    What Young Hearts Can Learn

    Prayer First

    Saint Charbel shows that real strength comes from daily prayer, not from noise or fame.

    Simple Work

    He worked with his hands, cared for the land, and offered ordinary tasks to God.

    Hidden Sacrifice

    He fasted, lived simply, and gave up comfort so his heart could belong fully to Jesus.

    Mercy for All

    His life still brings comfort, healing, and hope to people of every place and background.

    True Dates in the Story

    • May 8, 1828: Birth of Youssef Antoun Makhlouf (Saint Charbel) in Bekaa Kafra, Lebanon.
    • 1851: At age 23, he enters the Lebanese Maronite Order (formation at Mayfouk, then Annaya).
    • 1853: He makes his monastic vows and receives the name Charbel.
    • July 23, 1859: He is ordained a Maronite priest.
    • 1859-1875: He lives 16 years in monastic community life at Annaya.
    • 1875: He begins hermitage life near Annaya, where he remains about 23 years.
    • December 24, 1898: He dies after falling gravely ill during the Divine Liturgy in December 1898.
    • December 5, 1965: Beatified by Pope Paul VI.
    • October 9, 1977: Canonized by Pope Paul VI.
    • 1993: The widely known healing testimony of Nohad El Shami is reported at Annaya.

    Note: Some beloved stories (such as the water-lamp event) are presented as monastic tradition and devotional memory, while canonization itself depends on the Church's formal investigation process.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Who is this storybook for?

    Children ages 5 to 12, reading on their own or listening with family. Every page has a Read Aloud button with gentle narration, so the book can be heard as well as read.

    Is the story of Saint Charbel true?

    Yes. The storybook retells documented events of his life - his birth in Bekaa Kafra in 1828, his years as a monk and hermit, and his death in 1898. Where the book shares a traditional account, such as the water lamp, it names it as a traditional account.

    What is Saint Charbel known for?

    He was a Lebanese monk and the hermit of Annaya, remembered for a hidden life of prayer and for the healings linked to his intercession after his death. The Church canonized him on October 9, 1977.

    How long is the story?

    The storybook has 27 pages. You can move through them with the Previous and Next buttons.

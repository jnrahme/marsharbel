Saint Jude: A Question at the Table

Jude brings an honest question to Jesus. Read his story together, then make room for a question of your own.

Short answer: This nine-page Saint Jude storybook for ages 6 to 12 follows Jude Thaddeus among the apostles and his question at the Last Supper, with Jesus' answer about love and making a home with God, following Benedict XVI's reading of John 14. It ends with a narrated family reflection.

Story Summary

This story follows Jude Thaddeus among the apostles, his question at the Last Supper, and the answer Jesus gives about love and making a home with God. It follows Benedict XVI's reading of John 14. The letter bearing Jude's name is discussed with its uncertain authorship clearly stated. Nine illustrated pages end with a narrated family reflection.

    Read the Bible and pray together

    With a trusted adult, read John 14:22-23 and Jude 20-22. Notice the question at the table, the answer about love, and the invitation to show mercy to people who doubt.

    The final page offers four points of reflection and an original family prayer. Children can bring difficult questions to a trusted adult. Prayer does not replace practical help or promise a particular outcome.

    About this story

    Illustrations are imaginative composites, not eyewitness reconstructions. Pages eight and nine show modern family reflection scenes, not Jude alive in a later century. This Catholic retelling follows Benedict XVI's identification of Jude Thaddeus in John 14; the USCCB note records a different scholarly identification. The letter's authorship remains uncertain. The final reflection is original, not historical dialogue.

    Sources: Benedict XVI: Simon and Jude, 11 October 2006; USCCB: John 14 and identity note; USCCB: Letter of Jude;

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Who was Saint Jude Thaddeus?

    One of the Twelve apostles, distinct from Judas Iscariot. Little is known about his early life. This story follows Benedict XVI's reading of his question at the Last Supper.

    Who is this storybook for?

    Children ages 6 to 12, reading or listening with family. Nine illustrated pages lead to four narrated points of reflection.

    Did Saint Jude write the Letter of Jude?

    Christians have long linked the letter with this apostle, but its authorship is uncertain. The story says this clearly rather than treating the attribution as proved.

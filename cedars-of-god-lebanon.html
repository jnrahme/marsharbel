The Cedars of God: Lebanon's Sacred Cedar Forest

The Cedars of God (Arz el-Rab) is Lebanon's oldest cedar grove, on Mount Makmel above Bsharri, at about 1,900 to 2,050 meters. The Lebanon cedar is named 103 times in the Bible. The grove and the Qadisha Valley below it are together a UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1998.

What the Cedars of God Are

Arz el-Rab means "Cedars of the Lord." The grove stands on Mount Makmel, between 1,900 and 2,050 meters, to the east of the village of Bcharré in North Lebanon. UNESCO calls it the last vestige of the antique forests of Lebanon and one of the rare sites where the Lebanon cedar (Cedrus libani) still grows.

Some of the trees here are more than 30 meters tall and about 2,000 years old, according to reporting in The National. The cedar sits at the center of Lebanon's national flag.

The Cedars in Scripture

The cedar of Lebanon is cited 103 times in the Bible, and in antiquity its wood was prized for building great religious buildings. Psalm 92 says the righteous will grow like a cedar of Lebanon. For Lebanese Christians the grove is a sacred place, and the old trees are a living link to the land of the Bible.

A Forest the Church Protects

The Maronite Church has long protected the grove. A small chapel stands in the middle of the grove, and a Mass is celebrated there each year to honor the cedars. The Associated Press reported in 2023 on a Feast of the Transfiguration gathering at a mid-19th-century chapel in the forest, where the Maronite Patriarch, Beshara al-Rai, preached and the people sang hymns in Arabic and Aramaic.

The Church keeps strict rules to protect the forest and keep building out of it. Kiosks, tourist shops and a large car park are set far away from the trees.

The Cedars, the Qadisha Valley and Saint Charbel

The Cedars overlook the Qadisha Valley, the "Holy Valley," where Maronite monks and hermits took refuge for centuries. UNESCO lists the valley and the cedar forest as one World Heritage Site. The cliffs of the valley hold the monasteries of Qannoubine and Qozhaya.

The Cedars are in the same mountains as Bekaa Kafra, the village where Saint Charbel was born in 1828, and our pilgrimage guide places the grove about an hour from the village. Pilgrims often add Bsharri and the Cedars to a week-long Charbel itinerary.

Caring for the Cedars

The long-term survival of the forest is in doubt as temperatures rise. Local environmentalists told the Associated Press in 2023 that warmer weather has brought larger colonies of pests that feed on the cedars' bark. A Bcharre environmental group has planted about 200,000 cedars at higher altitudes over the years, and about 180,000 of them survived.

Visiting the Cedars

Bsharri is the nearest town and the usual base for the Cedars and the upper Qadisha Valley. Spring through autumn is the best season: winter snow can close the roads up to the Cedars, as our pilgrimage guide notes. Check conditions with local hosts before you drive up.

The grove is a protected forest and a place of prayer for many visitors. Walk quietly, keep to the paths, and leave the trees as you found them.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the Cedars of God?

The last great grove of ancient Lebanon cedars, on Mount Makmel above Bsharri in North Lebanon. In Arabic it is Arz el-Rab, "Cedars of the Lord."

How high is the Cedars of God forest?

UNESCO places it between 1,900 and 2,050 meters above sea level, east of the village of Bcharré.

How old are the cedars?

Some of the trees are reported to be more than 30 meters tall and about 2,000 years old. Counts and ages vary between sources, so treat them as estimates.

Why are the cedars sacred?

The Lebanon cedar is named 103 times in the Bible, and the Maronite Church has protected the grove, with a chapel in it and a Mass each year to honor the trees.

Is the Cedars of God a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

Yes. UNESCO inscribed the Qadisha Valley and the Forest of the Cedars of God together as one site in 1998.

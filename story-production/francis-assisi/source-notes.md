# Francis of Assisi: script checkpoint and sources

Original nine narrative pages plus four reflections. A composite chapel and path scene are explicitly labeled; no invented dialogue. The historical illness in the leper episode is not portrayed as contagious danger a child must confront alone; medical and trusted-adult guidance is explicit. War/encounter with Sultan is not offered as a child model for hazardous travel.

- Pope Benedict XVI, general audience, 27 Jan 2010, https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2010/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20100127.html . Fetched 28 Sep 2026. Supports Assisi upbringing, cloth merchant father, captivity and illness, encounter with a leper, San Damiano call and repair, family conflict, Gospel call and companions, 1209 Rome approval, Clare, Sultan meeting, Canticle, 1226 death. The text distinguishes reported religious call from physical repair and later broader mission.
- Franciscan Media, Jack Wintz, OFM, "St. Francis and the Birds" (2020), https://www.franciscanmedia.org/st-anthony-messenger/st-francis-and-the-birds/ . Fetched 28 Sep 2026. Describes Thomas of Celano and Bonaventure narratives of preaching to birds; the script marks the episode as an account rather than a verified zoological event.
- Thomas of Celano's *First Life*, excerpt hosted by the Holy See, https://www.vatican.va/spirit/documents/spirit_20001103_tom-da-celano_en.html . Search identified as primary early hagiographical account for leper encounter; Benedict's fetched audience is the direct source used for this script.

Do not paint the leprosy scene as horror, the sultan as a villain, or a bird scene as a zoological claim. The quoted title *Canticle of the Creatures* names his text, not copied song lyrics. No miracle outcome promised.

Francis of Assisi: The Joy of a Smaller Road

Around 1182, in Assisi, a hill town in Italy, Francis was born into a family with plenty. His father sold cloth. As a young man, Francis dreamed of brave adventures and wanted to be a knight. He went to war, was captured, and became sick. After he was freed, he returned to Assisi. The life he had planned was beginning to change.

Francis's change took time. One day he met a man living with leprosy, an illness that caused people to be feared and pushed away. Early accounts tell how Francis overcame his fear and greeted the man with peace. The person others avoided was someone Francis could welcome. His life began turning toward people who needed care. That change would also bring him to a small, damaged chapel.

Near Assisi stood a chapel called San Damiano. Francis prayed there before a crucifix. Christian accounts tell how he understood Christ calling him to repair the Church. He began by working on the building that needed repair. Over time, the call became larger than its walls. Francis was turning toward a life of serving God and caring for people.

Francis chose to give up the wealth he could have inherited. His father disagreed, and they had a painful conflict. Francis continued with his choice to live simply and follow Jesus. He would no longer build his life around the possessions his family could give him. This was an adult decision with a real cost. A new way of living was taking shape.

In 1208, Francis heard a Gospel passage about Jesus sending his followers to preach. He felt called to share the good news without gathering riches. Other men joined him. They became the Friars Minor, or little brothers. They prayed, traveled, worked, and spoke about peace and repentance, turning back toward God. Francis now had companions on the road he had chosen.

In 1209, Francis went to Rome to explain their way of life to Pope Innocent the Third. The pope gave his approval, and the brothers' community grew. Clare of Assisi followed a related path. Women formed the community later known as the Poor Clares. What began with Francis's choices was becoming a shared life. More people were bringing their work and prayer to it.

Francis delighted in the world around him. An early account tells of him stopping to preach to birds. This is a story passed down about Francis, remembered in paintings and books. He also praised the sun, moon, wind, water, and earth in his Canticle of the Creatures. He called creatures brothers and sisters. His care for neighbors reached outward in praise for the world God had made.

In 1219, during a war between Christians and Muslims, Francis traveled to Egypt to meet Sultan al-Malik al-Kamil. The accounts say the sultan welcomed him and they spoke together. Francis had crossed a divide between people at war. The war did not end with that meeting, but a conversation had taken place. Later, Francis returned to Italy and continued his life among the brothers.

Francis died in 1226 and was later named a saint. The young man who had hoped to be a knight had taken another road. He repaired a chapel, gathered companions, cared about people who were poor, and praised creation. The communities begun during his life continued after him. In Assisi and far beyond it, people still read his words and follow his way of prayer and service.

Francis met a person others avoided and later spoke with a ruler across a divide. Which meeting stayed with you, and why? Talk about it with someone who cares for you.

Read Matthew, chapter five, verse nine together. Jesus calls peacemakers blessed. Francis's meeting with the sultan gives us one moment to consider alongside that teaching.

Try a small act of peace today. Listen without interrupting, or include someone in a game. If someone is being hurt, ask a trusted adult for help.

God, help me see the people I overlook. Teach me to listen, share, and care for your creation. Saint Francis, pray for us. Amen.

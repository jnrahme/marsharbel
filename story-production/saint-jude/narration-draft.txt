Saint Jude: A Question at the Table

Page 1 - A name to know
In the first century, in the land where Jesus lived, Jude became one of his apostles. An apostle was someone Jesus chose to follow him and share his teaching. We call him Jude Thaddeus. He was not Judas Iscariot, another member of the Twelve. Little is known about Jude's early life, but one question he asked has stayed with the Church.

Page 2 - A place among twelve
Jude belonged to a group of people with different backgrounds. Jesus called them to follow him together. He had a place among the Twelve, alongside people whose stories are told in more detail. One of those remembered moments would be his question at the table.

Page 3 - Learning together
At the Last Supper, Jude was with the other disciples listening to Jesus. Jesus spoke about showing himself to those who loved him. Jude had a question about the people beyond that room.

Page 4 - An honest question
Jude asked why Jesus would show himself to his followers and not to the world. The Gospel of John keeps his question for us. He brought it to Jesus in the middle of their shared meal. Jesus answered him, but the answer was about more than who would see him. It began with love.

Page 5 - Jesus answers
Jesus spoke of loving him and keeping his word. He said that he and the Father would make their home with the person who loved him. Jude had asked about the world. Jesus answered with the closeness of a home. In John 14:22-23, that closeness belongs with love lived out, not simply with seeing something extraordinary.

Page 6 - A quiet kind of courage
Jude brought his question to Jesus, and Jesus answered with love and closeness to God. Later Christians would return to that exchange as they read the Gospel of John. Jude's question had become part of the Church's Scripture.

Page 7 - A letter that bears his name
A short letter in the Bible bears Jude's name. Christians have long linked it with this apostle, though we do not know for certain who wrote it. Its closing encouragement calls believers to pray, remain in God's love, and show mercy to people who doubt.

Page 8 - A friend to people who hope
Over the centuries, Christians have asked Saint Jude to pray for them in difficult times. People bring hopes that matter deeply to them. When you need help, you can pray and speak to a trusted adult.

Page 9 - Room for your question
Jude's name is still remembered, along with the question he brought to Jesus. His place at the table did not depend on already having every answer. The Gospel keeps both his question and Jesus's answer together: a question about the world, an answer beginning with love.

Points of reflection
What would you want to ask Jesus if you were at that table? You do not need to hurry toward an answer.

Read Jude 20-22 with a parent or another trusted adult. The passage connects prayer and God's love with mercy toward people who doubt.

Today, listen to someone's question without laughing at it. You can also bring one of your own questions to someone who cares for you.

God, help us come to you honestly and treat one another with mercy. Saint Jude, pray for us as we learn to follow Jesus. Amen.

# Saint Jude Thaddeus - sources and distinctions
Draft checkpoint 2026-09-28. Nine pages and four-point reflection. Original child-friendly prose. Imaginative scene on page 3 is explicitly composite. No invented dialogue or unsupported apostolic travel/martyrdom details.

- Benedict XVI, General Audience "Simon and Jude" (11 October 2006): https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2006/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20061011.html . Primary Holy See teaching: Jude Thaddeus distinct from Judas Iscariot; names in Matthew/Mark versus Luke/Acts; little information; Jude's Last Supper question and Jesus's answer; Letter attributed to Jude with caveat on paternity, call to mercy and prayer. Also discusses diversity of the Twelve.
- John 14:22-23 is quoted and interpreted in the Holy See audience. Script paraphrases rather than writing new words for Jude or Jesus. Lists of apostles Matthew 10:3-4, Mark 3:18, Luke 6:15-16, Acts 1:13 cited by that address. Letter of Jude 20-22 likewise cited there.

Editorial caution: Saying "the Church understands these as the apostle Jude Thaddeus" follows Benedict's explanation, but not a claim that all scholars resolve the identity the same way. The attribution of the letter is explicitly qualified. Popular patronage for difficult situations is devotional practice, not a promise of outcome; page 8 avoids a miracle claim. No documented childhood scenes, exact mission route, death scene, or fictitious conversations.

Scene-first opening update 2026-09-30: In the first century, in the land where Jesus lived, Jude was one of the apostles who followed him.

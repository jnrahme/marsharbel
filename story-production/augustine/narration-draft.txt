Around the year 354, in the town of Tagaste in North Africa, a boy named Augustine was born. His mother, Monica, was a Christian. His father, Patricius, was not yet one. Augustine was clever and curious. He loved words and big questions. This is the story of how a boy with a restless heart found the One he was looking for.

PAGE 2
Monica loved her son and wanted him to know God. She prayed for him, and she kept praying for many years. Augustine did not always listen to her. When he was sixteen, he and some friends shook a pear tree near their vineyard late at night and took the fruit. He later wrote that he already had better fruit, and that he did not even want the pears.

PAGE 3
Augustine left home to study in the big city of Carthage. He became a teacher of public speaking, and people admired him. But in his heart something was missing. He read many books and tried many ideas. Years later, he wrote about this search. He said that our hearts are restless until they rest in God.

PAGE 4
Augustine moved across the sea to the city of Milan, in Italy. There he heard a bishop named Ambrose preach. Ambrose spoke about the Bible in a way Augustine had never heard. Augustine began to listen with open ears. His questions did not vanish, but he started to think that the Christian faith might be true.

PAGE 5
It was still hard. Augustine wanted to change, yet he kept putting it off. He later wrote about a day in a garden in Milan. He threw himself down under a fig tree, and he cried out to God, "How long? Tomorrow, and tomorrow? Why not now?" If you have ever known you should do something good and kept saying "tomorrow," you may know how that feels.

PAGE 6
Then, Augustine later wrote, he heard a child's voice from a nearby house, chanting again and again, "Take up and read." He took the words as a call to open the Bible. He read the first lines he saw, and they called him to leave his old ways and follow Jesus. Augustine said yes. He was about thirty-one.

PAGE 7
At Easter, in the year 387, Bishop Ambrose baptized Augustine. Augustine was thirty-two, and his mother Monica was with him. On the journey home to Africa, Monica became ill and died in the port town of Ostia. Augustine was very sad, and he thanked God for his mother's life.

PAGE 8
Augustine went back to Africa. He lived a simple life of prayer with friends. In the town of Hippo, he became a priest, and later their bishop. He was bishop for about thirty-five years. He preached, he helped the poor, and he wrote many books.

PAGE 9
In 430, enemy forces surrounded Hippo, and Augustine grew ill. His biographer Possidius says Augustine asked for the penitential psalms to be written on the wall, where he could read them. Augustine died on August 28, not yet seventy-six. The Church remembers him as a saint. People still read his books and ask his questions today.

PAGE 10
What do people look for when they want to feel happy or at peace? Would you like to read a Bible passage together with a parent or another trusted adult? It is in the Book of Jeremiah, chapter twenty-nine, verse thirteen. It says: "You shall seek me, and shall find me: when you shall seek me with all your heart." This week, try one small act. Take a few quiet minutes, away from screens, and tell God about one big question you have. A short prayer: Dear God, thank you for being near. Help me to look for you with all my heart. Saint Augustine, pray for us. Amen.

# Anthony of Padua: script checkpoint and sources

Original nine-page narration with four-point reflection. Scenes introduced with "imagine" are composites; no invented historical dialogue or guaranteed miracle. Child illness requires rest, trusted adults, medical care. Lost person safety overrides the familiar "lost things" devotion.

- Pope Benedict XVI, general audience, 10 Feb 2010, https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2010/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20100210.html . Fetched 28 Sep 2026. Supports Fernando's Lisbon birth c.1195, Augustinian study in Lisbon/Coimbra, influence of five Moroccan Franciscan martyrs, renaming and Morocco trip, illness and return to Italy, meeting Francis in 1221, Forli preaching, ministry and theology teaching, Padua, death in 1231, canonization in 1232, sermons and prayer. Pope's account does not document private speech or every encounter.
- Pope Benedict XVI, general audience, 27 Jan 2010, https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2010/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20100127.html . Context for the Friars Minor and Francis's simple Gospel life. Fetched 28 Sep 2026.

The custom of asking Anthony's help for lost objects is mentioned only as present devotional practice, not as a proved event in his lifetime or a promised outcome. Francis's letter of approval is reported by Benedict; not dramatized as a dialogue.

Opening setting update (30 Sep 2026): Our story begins around 1195, in Lisbon, Portugal. Source: the Benedict XVI audience cited above. Francis birth is dated late 1181 or early 1182; Anthony birth is circa 1195. Approximate phrasing preserves that uncertainty.

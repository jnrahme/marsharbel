# Therese of Lisieux: script checkpoint and sources

Nine narrative pages plus four-point reflection. Original child-friendly narration, no invented Therese speech or guaranteed roses/miracles. Two everyday convent scenes explicitly called composites. Loss is not minimized; illness gets adult care, rest and medical treatment rather than an invitation to suffer.

- Holy See, *The Life of Saint Thérèse of Lisieux*, https://www.vatican.va/news_services/liturgy/saints/ns_lit_doc_19101997_stherese_en.html . Fetched 28 Sep 2026. Supports 1873 Alencon birth to Louis and Zelie, mother death in 1877, move to Lisieux, Pauline/Marie in Carmel, meeting Pope Leo XIII in 1887, entry 1888 at age fifteen, father's illness and 1894 death, guidance of novices, missionary correspondence/prayers, manuscripts, final illness, infirmary and 1897 death; 1925 canonization, 1997 Doctor of the Church. The biography reports private spiritual experiences; script omits miraculous healing claim and does not treat roses as a guaranteed signal.
- Pope Francis, *C'est la confiance* (2023), https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/apost_exhortations/documents/20231015-santateresa-delbambinogesu.html . Fetched 28 Sep 2026. Paragraphs 14-21 explain her little way as trust and love, for everyone, not merit-by-effort. Supports the narration's distinction between small daily acts and a magic formula.

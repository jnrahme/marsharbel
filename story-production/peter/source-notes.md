# Saint Peter storybook research and editorial notes

This is an original child-suitable retelling drawn from Holy See catecheses and the New Testament, not a copied transcript or a claim of eyewitness detail beyond those sources. It is a script checkpoint for review; no art or narration is yet published. Quoted speech is Scripture wording quoted through the Holy See catecheses or the NABRE text, never invented dialogue. Peter's denial is told honestly because it is the heart of the story's lesson; the failure is framed with hope, not shame. The martyrdom is stated without graphic description. The name meaning (Cephas = rock) comes from the Holy See catechesis itself.

- Pope Benedict XVI, General Audience, 17 May 2006, "Peter, the fisherman": https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2006/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20060517.html . Supports: Simon son of John, from Bethsaida, brother of Andrew, small family fishing business on the Lake of Gennesaret with Zebedee's family, married with mother-in-law in the Capernaum house, a believing Jew who longed for God's action, the call scene with the crowd, teaching from the boat, "Master, we toiled all night and took nothing! But at your word I will let down the nets" (Lk 5:4-5), the miraculous catch, "Depart from me, for I am a sinful man" (Lk 5:8, narrated here without the quote), "Do not be afraid; henceforth you will be catching men" (Lk 5:10), the names Cephas/Petros "rock", and the confession "You are the Christ" at Caesarea Philippi (Mk 8:29).
- Pope Benedict XVI, General Audience, 24 May 2006, "Peter, the Apostle": https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2006/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20060524.html . Supports: the multiplication of the loaves and the hard bread-of-life teaching, many disciples leaving, "Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life" (Jn 6:66-69), the denial and the weeping repentance (Mk 14:66-72; Mt 26:75), the risen Jesus' threefold "Do you love me?" and "Feed my sheep" mission by the Lake of Tiberias, and the foreshadowing that his life would be "sealed with martyrdom".
- Acts 2, NABRE (USCCB): https://bible.usccb.org/bible/acts/2 . Supports: the Holy Spirit's coming with "a noise like a strong driving wind", Peter standing to address the crowds, and about three thousand baptized that day (Acts 2:41).
- Acts 12, NABRE (USCCB): https://bible.usccb.org/bible/acts/12 . Supports: Herod's arrest of Peter during Passover, four squads of soldiers, the Church's prayer, the angel, chains falling from his wrists, the iron gate opening by itself, the house of Mary mother of John Mark, and Rhoda's overjoyed failure to open the gate.
- Eusebius, Church History 3.1-2 (quoting Origen), via New Advent: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm . Supports: Peter's coming to Rome, his crucifixion under Nero, and the head-downwards tradition (retold here only as "he did not consider himself worthy to die in exactly the same way as Jesus", without physical description). This is ancient tradition, flagged as "an ancient history tells us".
- Fabbrica di San Pietro (Holy See), "The Necropolis": https://www.basilicasanpietro.va/en/san-pietro/the-necropolis . Supports: the Vatican Necropolis beneath Saint Peter's Basilica, the ancient road leading to the tomb of the Prince of the Apostles, and the excavations undertaken under Pius XII.

Editorial judgments: Peter's inner states are limited to what the catecheses and Gospel texts support (amazement, fear, repentance); "A name like that is a promise" is presented as narrative reflection, not fact. "Courage is not something you are born with" is the author's pastoral summary. The reflection page is an authored pastoral application (God in ordinary work, trust amid hard questions, truth-telling after mistakes, courage grown by practice), not a quotation from Peter. Child safety: denial is normalized as human failure with a path back through truth and trusted people; martyrdom is one sentence without method detail beyond the ancient tradition's framing; the angel scene carries the joy of the story. No miracles are promised to the child; Gospel and Acts events are presented as what Scripture records. Independent intake correction (2026-09-30): Eusebius supports Peter's requested head-down crucifixion but does not state the unworthiness motive. Jerome, On Illustrious Men, chapter1, explicitly says he was "unworthy to be crucified in the same manner as his Lord": https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2708.htm . Keep this as ancient tradition, not Gospel evidence.

The Massabki Brothers: Three Lives of Courage

Page 1 - Three brothers in Damascus
In the busy streets of nineteenth-century Damascus lived three Maronite Christian brothers: Francis, Mooti, and Raphael Massabki. Francis and Mooti had families of their own. Raphael, the youngest, helped his family and his community. They were not kings or soldiers. They were ordinary people with work to do, people to love, and a faith that helped guide their days.

Page 2 - Francis's open hands
Francis traded silk. He was known for honesty and for sharing with people who needed help. A merchant must count carefully, but generosity is more than counting. Picture a colorful market full of folded cloth and neighbors greeting one another. That picture is a storytelling composite, not a record of any one morning. Francis's life shows that business and kindness need not be strangers.

Page 3 - Mooti's lessons
Mooti visited the Franciscan friary often. He prayed there and helped teach boys at the local school. He and his wife were raising five children. A lesson is more than words on a page. Children learn by seeing how adults care, listen, and show up again tomorrow. Mooti made room in his life for both his family and the children learning at the friary.

Page 4 - Raphael's quiet help
Raphael helped his family and the friars. He was especially devoted to Mary and spent time praying at the friary church. Sometimes helping means being ready for a needed task. Sometimes it means staying close to the people you love. The sources tell us less about Raphael than about his brothers. We will not invent the missing details. His life still belonged to this story.

Page 5 - The friary door
The brothers knew the Franciscan friars of St Paul in Damascus. Eight friars lived and served there. The Massabki brothers were laypeople: they belonged to the Church, but they were not friars themselves. They joined in a shared life of prayer and service. Imagine a door open to people arriving for help and a place to pray. This scene is a composite, not a report of a particular gathering.

Page 6 - A frightening night
In July 1860, violence reached Damascus. Christian families faced danger. The brothers and friars were at the friary on the night of July 9 to 10. This is a sad part of the story. If you feel afraid as you listen, pause and talk with a trusted adult. The violence was done by people, not by everyone who belonged to a different faith or community. We can tell the truth without teaching hatred.

Page 7 - Courage without fighting
Attackers entered the friary. The brothers were told to give up their Christian faith. They did not. They and the friars were killed. We do not show the violence in this book or put made-up speeches in their mouths. The three brothers did not seek danger. Their courage was faithfulness in a terrible moment, not a lesson that children should face danger alone. When you are in danger, move toward safety and call for adult help.

Page 8 - Names kept together
The friars and three brothers were buried together. Families and communities remembered Francis, Mooti, and Raphael by name. Some names are easier to find in old records than others; we remember all three, not only the eldest. In 2024 the Church named the brothers saints along with the eight friars. A saint's story can remember grief honestly while also asking us to choose service and peace.

Page 9 - The peace we choose
Francis shared, Mooti taught, and Raphael helped and prayed. Each brother had his own way of loving others. Today we cannot undo that frightening night. We can refuse to hate a whole group for the acts of attackers. We can make a safer world by learning our neighbors' names, offering help, telling the truth, and asking trusted adults to help when someone is threatened. Courage can begin with peace.

Four things to remember
1. Each brother's ordinary service mattered.
2. We remember violence honestly without blaming a whole community.
3. Children in danger should seek safety and trusted adults, never imitate martyrdom.
4. Choose one peaceful act of courage or kindness today.

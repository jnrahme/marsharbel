Saint Charbel Oil: What It Is and How to Pray With It

Small vials of blessed oil, given freely at the shrine in Annaya and carried to every continent - what the oil actually is, the story behind the devotion, how pilgrims pray with it, and the honest line between a sacrament and a sacramental.

Prayer for Healing Story of the Tomb Visit Annaya

What Is Saint Charbel Oil?

Saint Charbel oil is olive oil blessed in honor of Saint Charbel, distributed at his shrine in Annaya, Lebanon - usually in small vials, often given freely. Pilgrims apply it while praying for healing: a small cross on the forehead or on the place that hurts, with a short prayer asking Saint Charbel's intercession. It is a devotional object, like a rosary or holy water - not a medicine, not a guarantee, and never a replacement for a doctor.

Its origin is tied to the story of his tomb. After Saint Charbel died in 1898, witnesses described a fluid - recorded as blood and water, sometimes described with an oil-like quality - exuding from his remarkably preserved body. The monks collected it on cloths, and that became the origin of the blessed oil devotion recorded in the tradition. Today the oil is blessed by the monks, commonly in contact with the saint's tomb and relics.

The Story Behind the Devotion

The best-known account is the healing of Nohad El Shami, a Lebanese mother of twelve left partially paralyzed by a stroke in January 1993. Her son brought her blessed oil and soil from Saint Charbel's tomb at Annaya; she was anointed with it - and, the account records, recovered, going on to visit the hermitage on the 22nd of every month until her death. That single story, told and retold, did more than any other to spread the oil devotion around the world. It is a witness account preserved in the tradition, and we report it as such - the fuller story is on our 22nd of the month page.

How Pilgrims Pray With the Oil

There is no required rite - this is a devotion, not a liturgy. The common practice, kept by families in Lebanon and across the diaspora:

  • Pray first. The oil is an occasion for prayer, not a substitute for it. Many begin with a prayer to Saint Charbel or a decade of the Rosary.
  • Anoint with a small sign of the cross - on your own forehead, or on the person you are praying for, or on the place of illness.
  • Ask for Saint Charbel's intercession in your own words, for healing or for the intention you carry - the healing prayer page has a full pattern.
  • Keep the rest with care, as you would any blessed object - not in a drawer of superstition, but as a reminder of a prayer you have made.

Some parishes, especially on the 22nd of the month and on his feast day, anoint the faithful with Saint Charbel oil communally after Mass.

A Sacrament, or a Sacramental? (Read Before You Use It)

An honest distinction matters here, because confusion on this point can quietly turn devotion into superstition. Saint Charbel oil is not a sacrament. The seven sacraments - including the Anointing of the Sick, a priest's sacramental anointing instituted by Christ - are actions of the Church that confer grace. Saint Charbel oil is a sacramental: a sacred sign, like holy water, a blessed rosary, or a medal, that the Church gives to dispose us to prayer and to God's grace through faith.

That means the oil has no power of its own. What matters is the prayer it accompanies and the God to whom the prayer is addressed. The Church has always been careful here: blessed objects are helps to faith, not talismans, and no oil - however holy the saint - can promise an outcome. If you are sick, see your doctor and, if you are Catholic, ask your priest for the Anointing of the Sick; Saint Charbel oil can accompany both, and replace neither.

Where to Get Saint Charbel Oil

The oil is distributed at the monastery of Saint Maron in Annaya, near the tomb - small bottles, traditionally given freely to pilgrims (see our pilgrim's guide). Many Lebanese and Maronite parishes abroad also keep it, especially after relic visits, and relics touring with oil have reached communities from Naples to the Americas. One honest caution: be wary of sellers online. A blessed thing sold at a markup, from an unknown source, is neither guaranteed genuine nor in the spirit in which the shrine gives it away.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Saint Charbel oil?

Olive oil blessed in honor of Saint Charbel, distributed at his shrine in Annaya, Lebanon. Pilgrims apply it with a sign of the cross while praying for healing. It is a devotional aid, not a medicine.

Is Saint Charbel oil a sacrament?

No. It is a sacramental - a blessed object like holy water or a rosary - that accompanies prayer. It is distinct from the sacrament of the Anointing of the Sick, which is administered by a priest.

How do you use Saint Charbel oil?

Pray first, then make a small sign of the cross with the oil on your forehead or on the person you pray for, asking Saint Charbel's intercession. There is no fixed rite; the prayer matters, not the oil itself.

Where can I get Saint Charbel oil?

At the monastery of Saint Maron in Annaya, near the tomb - small bottles traditionally given freely - and through many Maronite parishes abroad. Be cautious of online sellers.

Does Saint Charbel oil heal?

The Church makes no such promise, and neither does this site. Healings have been reported by pilgrims who used it in prayer - we record those accounts as testimony. The oil is an occasion for faith, not a guaranteed outcome, and never a substitute for medical care.

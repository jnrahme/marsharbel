How This Library Is Sourced and Labeled

Every prayer here is either a traditional text long used in the Church, Scripture quoted from the public-domain Douay-Rheims Bible (Challoner revision), or a brief cited reference to an official Church document such as the Catechism of the Catholic Church (cited by paragraph number rather than reproduced). We do not copy modern copyrighted prayer books or translations. Where a prayer is traditional and its English rendering varies between communities, we say so.

Every entry also carries an honest status line. "Church-attested" means an official act of the Church, such as an indulgence grant in the Enchiridion Indulgentiarum issued by the Apostolic Penitentiary. "Devotional tradition" means a practice or belief handed on by the faithful without that official weight. No prayer, novena, or devotion mechanically guarantees healing, money, a miracle, or any other specific outcome: prayer asks, and God remains free. Where a text itself speaks with confidence (as the Memorare does), we label that confidence as devotional, not as a Church guarantee.

Maronite & Syriac Tradition

Qadeeshat Aloho (Trisagion)

Holy are You, O God. Holy are You, O Strong One. Holy are You, O Immortal One. Have mercy on us.

In Syriac tradition: Qadeeshat Aloho, Qadeeshat Hayeltono, Qadeeshat Lo Moyouto, etraham 'lain.

Purpose
Adoration: the thrice-holy praise of God at the heart of Syriac worship, echoing the angels' cry in Isaiah 6:3.
How to pray
Sing or recite it slowly, standing, letting each of the three acclamations land before the plea for mercy.
When & how long
Sung in the Maronite Qurbono (Divine Liturgy), especially before the Gospel, and fitting any time as a short act of adoration; under a minute.
Source & tradition
Ancient Trisagion hymn of the Syriac churches, prayed in the Maronite liturgy for well over a millennium. Traditional; English renderings vary slightly between eparchies.
Spiritual fruit
Reverence and recollection: tradition understands it as joining the worship of heaven rather than asking for anything.

Lenten Prayer of Saint Ephrem

O Lord and Master of my life, keep from me the spirit of indifference and discouragement, lust for power and idle chatter. Instead, grant to me, Your servant, the spirit of wholeness of being, humble-mindedness, patience, and love. O Lord and King, grant me the grace to be aware of my sins and not to judge my brother; for You are blessed, now and ever and forever. Amen.

Purpose
Repentance: it asks freedom from indifference, love of power, idle talk, and judging others, and asks for humility, patience, and love in their place.
How to pray
Slowly, phrase by phrase. In Syriac and Byzantine practice it is prayed with a prostration or deep bow after each petition; kneeling is a fitting substitute.
When & how long
Daily during the Great Lent, morning and evening in Eastern usage; about two to three minutes, longer with prostrations.
Source & tradition
Attributed to Saint Ephrem the Syrian (d. 373), a deacon and doctor of the Church honored deeply in the Maronite tradition. Traditional English rendering.
Spiritual fruit
Exactly what it petitions: self-knowledge, humility, patience, and freedom from judging others. Tradition treats it as the Church's school of Lent, not as a transaction.

From the Hymns of Saint Ephrem

"There came out from Him water and blood; Adam washed and lived and returned to Paradise." And the refrain: "Blessed is He Who has quickened the dead of Sheol by His Cross!"

Purpose
Meditation on the Cross and on baptism: the water and blood from Christ's side washing humanity back into Paradise.
How to pray
Read it slowly as lectio divina, then repeat the refrain several times and sit with it.
When & how long
Fitting in Lent, Holy Week, and Eastertide, and at baptisms; five unhurried minutes.
Source & tradition
Saint Ephrem the Syrian, Nisibene Hymns (Death and Satan before the Cross), public-domain English translation in Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Series II, Vol. XIII (1898).
Spiritual fruit
Gratitude for baptism and hope in the resurrection, in the imagery of the greatest Syriac poet-theologian.

Maronite Morning Offering (Traditional)

I offer You, O Lord, this day: my prayers, works, joys, and sufferings. May they rise before You like incense, through the intercession of the Mother of God and all the saints. Keep me in Your grace, and let Your will be done in me. Amen.

Purpose
To consecrate the whole day, including its work and its sufferings, before it begins.
How to pray
On waking, before the phone and before work. Many families pray it aloud together.
When & how long
Every morning; about a minute.
Source & tradition
Traditional morning offering as prayed in Maronite households; the exact formula varies by family and parish.
Spiritual fruit
Intention: the day is handed over before it can take over. A devotional tradition, not a formula that binds the day to go well.

Core Catholic Prayers

Our Father

Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be Thy name; Thy kingdom come; Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread; and forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

Purpose
The prayer Christ himself taught: praise of the Father first, then petitions for bread, forgiveness, and deliverance. The Catechism calls it "the summary of the whole Gospel" (CCC 2761).
How to pray
Slowly, as a child speaking to a father. It opens every decade of the Rosary and anchors the Mass.
When & how long
Daily: in the liturgy, the Rosary, and morning and evening prayer; under a minute.
Source & tradition
Matthew 6:9-13 (also Luke 11:2-4); traditional liturgical English.
Spiritual fruit
Filial trust: the Catechism teaches that this prayer forms us to ask as children, not as claimants (CCC 2777-2785).

Hail Mary

Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

Purpose
Praise of Mary in the words of Scripture, then a petition for her intercession now and at death.
How to pray
Unhurriedly. In the Rosary, each Hail Mary is prayed while meditating on the mystery of its decade.
When & how long
In the Rosary and the Angelus, and any time; seconds, or about twenty minutes for five decades.
Source & tradition
Luke 1:28 and 1:42 with the traditional closing petition, fixed in common use by the 16th century.
Spiritual fruit
Marian trust and humility. On the Rosary as a whole, see the Rosary guide; its official indulgence grant is noted under the psalms and Scripture entries below.

Glory Be

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit; as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

Purpose
Doxology: pure praise of the Trinity, asking nothing.
How to pray
Simply and attentively; it closes each Rosary decade and many devotions.
When & how long
Through the day and in every Rosary; seconds.
Source & tradition
Traditional doxology (the Gloria Patri) of the early Church.
Spiritual fruit
A habit of praise: it recenters prayer on God rather than on our requests.

Memorare

Remember, O most gracious Virgin Mary, that never was it known that anyone who fled to thy protection, implored thy help, or sought thy intercession was left unaided. Inspired by this confidence, I fly unto thee, O Virgin of virgins, my mother; to thee do I come, before thee I stand, sinful and sorrowful. O Mother of the Word Incarnate, despise not my petitions, but in thy mercy hear and answer me. Amen.

Purpose
An urgent appeal for Mary's intercession in need.
How to pray
Once, slowly, naming the need; traditionally repeated across the nine days of a novena.
When & how long
In urgent need, and at the hour of death; about a minute.
Source & tradition
Traditional; its popular form is credited to Father Claude Bernard (17th century) and it is often linked in devotion with Saint Bernard of Clairvaux.
Spiritual fruit
Confidence in approaching God through Mary's prayers.
Promises & status
The line "never was it known" is devotional confidence, not a Church guarantee that every petition is granted as asked. The Church grants a partial indulgence for its devout recitation (Enchiridion Indulgentiarum, Apostolic Penitentiary) - a Church-attested grant about the prayer, not a promise about outcomes.

Anima Christi

Soul of Christ, sanctify me. Body of Christ, save me. Blood of Christ, inebriate me. Water from the side of Christ, wash me. Passion of Christ, strengthen me. O good Jesus, hear me. Within Thy wounds hide me. Suffer me not to be separated from Thee. From the malignant enemy defend me. In the hour of my death call me, and bid me come unto Thee, that with Thy saints I may praise Thee for ever and ever. Amen.

Purpose
Thanksgiving and union with Christ's Passion, above all after receiving him in the Eucharist.
How to pray
Silently and slowly, line by line, kneeling after Communion.
When & how long
Immediately after Holy Communion; also in Passion meditation during Lent. Two to three minutes.
Source & tradition
Traditional; a 14th-century prayer long used in the Latin Church.
Spiritual fruit
Gratitude and a felt nearness to the Passion, the school of self-giving love.
Promises & status
Church-attested: a partial indulgence is granted for its devout recitation (Enchiridion Indulgentiarum, Apostolic Penitentiary).

Act of Contrition (Traditional)

O my God, I am heartily sorry for having offended Thee, and I detest all my sins because of Thy just punishments, but most of all because they offend Thee, my God, who art all-good and deserving of all my love. I firmly resolve, with the help of Thy grace, to sin no more and to avoid the near occasions of sin. Amen.

Purpose
To express sorrow for sin and a resolve to change, resting on God's mercy.
How to pray
After a quiet examination of conscience, slowly and meaning the resolution; aloud within the Sacrament of Penance when the priest invites it.
When & how long
In nightly examination of conscience and in Confession; about a minute.
Source & tradition
Traditional form; on contrition itself see the Catechism, CCC 1451-1454.
Spiritual fruit
Contrition: "sorrow of the soul and detestation for the sin committed, together with the resolution not to sin again" (Council of Trent, cited in CCC 1451).

Come, Holy Spirit

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Thy faithful and enkindle in them the fire of Thy love. Send forth Thy Spirit and they shall be created, and Thou shalt renew the face of the earth. Let us pray: O God, who by the light of the Holy Spirit did instruct the hearts of the faithful, grant that by the same Holy Spirit we may be truly wise and ever enjoy His consolations. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

Purpose
To ask the Holy Spirit for wisdom and right judgment before acting.
How to pray
Once, slowly, before the task: study, a decision, a difficult conversation, or work.
When & how long
Any time guidance is needed; especially fitting at Pentecost. About a minute.
Source & tradition
Traditional invocation from the Pentecost liturgy of the Roman Missal.
Spiritual fruit
Docility: the habit of asking before deciding.
Promises & status
Church-attested: a partial indulgence is granted for this prayer (Enchiridion Indulgentiarum, Apostolic Penitentiary). The richer plenary grant on January 1 and Pentecost attaches to the Veni Creator hymn, a different text.

Prayer to Saint Michael the Archangel

Saint Michael the Archangel, defend us in battle; be our protection against the wickedness and snares of the devil. May God rebuke him, we humbly pray; and do thou, O Prince of the Heavenly Host, by the power of God, cast into hell Satan and all the evil spirits who prowl about the world seeking the ruin of souls. Amen.

Purpose
To ask God's protection against spiritual evil through Saint Michael's intercession.
How to pray
Once, attentively; many parishes pray it together after Mass.
When & how long
After Mass, in temptation or spiritual distress, and any time; under a minute.
Source & tradition
Composed by Pope Leo XIII in 1884 for the Leonine Prayers after Mass.
Spiritual fruit
Confidence that evil is resisted by God's power, not ours.
Promises & status
Devotional tradition only: no indulgence attaches to this prayer in the current Enchiridion, and it is a prayer of petition, not an exorcism or a guarantee of protection from harm.

Prayers by Need

For Healing

Lord Jesus, You healed the sick who came to You in faith. Look on this illness with mercy. Give strength to the suffering, wisdom to those who care for them, and peace to those who wait. If it be Your will, restore health; in all things, keep us close to You. Amen.

Purpose
Intercession for the sick: strength for the suffering, wisdom for caregivers, and healing if God wills it.
How to pray
Slowly, naming the person. It can be prayed daily through an illness, and joined to the Saint Charbel healing prayers.
When & how long
In illness, before surgery, and while waiting on diagnoses; a minute, daily if needed.
Source & tradition
Traditional intercessory form.
Spiritual fruit
Peace and solidarity: the sick are prayed with, not prayed at.
Promises & status
No prayer mechanically guarantees healing. The Catechism is explicit: "even the most intense prayers do not always obtain the healing of all illnesses" (CCC 1508). The Church's sacrament for the sick is the Anointing of the Sick (CCC 1499-1532); prayer supports, it does not control.

In Anxiety

"Be nothing solicitous: but in every thing, by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your petitions be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasseth all understanding, keep your hearts and minds in Christ Jesus." (Philippians 4:6-7, Douay-Rheims)

Purpose
To hand worry over to God and receive his peace.
How to pray
Read the passage slowly two or three times, then name the worry to God plainly and sit quietly.
When & how long
In moments of worry, and as a morning or evening anchor; five minutes.
Source & tradition
Scripture, public-domain Douay-Rheims Bible (Challoner revision).
Spiritual fruit
The passage itself names the fruit: "the peace of God, which surpasseth all understanding." That is Scripture's own promise, received by faith, not a technique with a timer.

In Grief

"The Lord is nigh unto them that are of a contrite heart: and he will save the humble of spirit." (Psalm 33:19, Douay-Rheims) Lord, hold the one we have lost in Your light, and hold us in Your peace. Amen.

Purpose
To grieve with God: entrusting the dead to him and asking peace for the living.
How to pray
Read the verse, then speak the name of the one who died, then the petition. Praying it with others helps.
When & how long
After a loss, at anniversaries, and in November, the Church's month of prayer for the dead; a few minutes.
Source & tradition
Scripture, public-domain Douay-Rheims Bible (Challoner); the closing petition is traditional.
Spiritual fruit
Consolation and hope. No devotion shortens grief on a schedule; the promise here is God's nearness, which the psalm itself attests.

In Gratitude

"Give praise to the Lord, for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever." (Psalm 117:1, Douay-Rheims) Thank You, Lord, for this day and its gifts, known and unknown. Amen.

Purpose
Thanksgiving: naming God's goodness before asking for anything else.
How to pray
Read the verse, then name three concrete gifts of the day aloud.
When & how long
Morning or evening, daily; a minute or two.
Source & tradition
Scripture, public-domain Douay-Rheims Bible (Challoner); the closing line is traditional.
Spiritual fruit
Joy and attentiveness: gratitude trains the eye to notice mercy.

By Season & Feast

Advent

Come, Lord Jesus. Prepare our hearts as You prepared a dwelling in the Virgin's womb. Teach us to wait in hope, with Mary, for Your coming. Amen.

Purpose
To learn waiting: preparing for Christmas by hoping with Mary.
How to pray
As a short daily aspiration through Advent, perhaps at an Advent wreath; pair with the Annunciation meditation.
When & how long
The four weeks before Christmas; seconds each day.
Source & tradition
Traditional Advent aspiration (cf. Revelation 22:20).
Spiritual fruit
Hopeful patience in a season built on delay.

The Great Lent

Pray the Lenten Prayer of Saint Ephrem (above) daily, and the Miserere: "Have mercy on me, O God, according to thy great mercy. And according to the multitude of thy tender mercies blot out my iniquity." (Psalm 50:3, Douay-Rheims)

Purpose
Repentance: Lent's daily return to God.
How to pray
The Saint Ephrem prayer each morning and evening, and the Miserere slowly, verse by verse, when conscience is heavy.
When & how long
Through the Great Lent, especially weekdays; a few minutes daily.
Source & tradition
St Ephrem prayer (traditional); Psalm 50 from the public-domain Douay-Rheims Bible.
Spiritual fruit
A clean heart, in the psalm's own words: honest repentance rather than vague regret.
Promises & status
Church-attested: a partial indulgence is granted for the devout recitation of the Miserere (Enchiridion Indulgentiarum, Apostolic Penitentiary).

Feast of Saint Charbel (July 24 in the Maronite calendar)

Pray the Saint Charbel intercession prayer and, if you can, the nine-day novena leading to July 24. The feast falls on the third Sunday of July in the Maronite liturgical calendar, observed widely on July 24.

Purpose
To honor Saint Charbel and ask his intercession, joining the worldwide devotion around his feast.
How to pray
The novena: the same prayer each day from July 15 to 23, a few minutes daily; on the feast itself, attend Mass if you can and pray the intercession prayer.
When & how long
Novena July 15-23; feast day July 24 (third Sunday of July in the Maronite calendar).
Source & tradition
Maronite liturgical calendar; novena practice is devotional tradition.
Spiritual fruit
Closeness to Saint Charbel's example of silence, prayer, and Eucharistic devotion.
Promises & status
Church-attested: the Church grants a partial indulgence to those who recite an approved prayer in honor of a saint on his feast day (Enchiridion Indulgentiarum, "Veneration of the Saints"). Healings attributed to Saint Charbel's intercession belong to devotional tradition and individual testimony; none is promised, and the Church investigates claims rigorously before calling anything miraculous.

Christmas (Season of the Glorious Birth)

"Glory to God in the highest: and on earth peace to men of good will." (Luke 2:14, Douay-Rheims) In the Maronite tradition, the Christmas season extends through Epiphany (Denho) on January 6.

Purpose
To adore the newborn Christ with the angels' own words.
How to pray
Sing or recite the Gloria verse at the crib or the tree, and linger over it rather than rushing to presents.
When & how long
December 25 through January 6 (Epiphany, Denho, in the Maronite calendar).
Source & tradition
Luke 2:14, public-domain Douay-Rheims Bible (Challoner revision); Maronite liturgical calendar.
Spiritual fruit
Joy: the season's whole point, received not manufactured.

By Sacrament

Before Holy Communion

Lord, I am not worthy that You should enter under my roof; but only say the word, and my soul shall be healed. (cf. Matthew 8:8, prayed at every Mass)

Purpose
Humility and readiness before receiving the Eucharist.
How to pray
Silently with the whole congregation just before receiving, then again quietly in your pew.
When & how long
At every Mass, immediately before Communion; seconds.
Source & tradition
The liturgy of the Mass, from the centurion's words in Matthew 8:8.
Spiritual fruit
Reverence: receiving as a gift, never as a right.

After Holy Communion

Pray the Anima Christi (above), the Church's classic thanksgiving after Communion.

Purpose
Thanksgiving while the gift is still warm.
How to pray
The Anima Christi slowly, kneeling, before the conversation and the parking lot reclaim you.
When & how long
In the quiet minutes after receiving; two to three minutes.
Source & tradition
Traditional, 14th century.
Spiritual fruit
Gratitude and recollection.
Promises & status
Church-attested: the Anima Christi carries a partial indulgence (Enchiridion Indulgentiarum, Apostolic Penitentiary).

Before Confession

Pray Come, Holy Spirit (above), then examine your conscience quietly and pray the Act of Contrition (above) before entering the Sacrament of Penance.

Purpose
Preparation: an honest conscience and a willing heart before the sacrament.
How to pray
Invoke the Holy Spirit, review the commandments and your life since your last confession, then pray the Act of Contrition.
When & how long
Before each confession; ten quiet minutes serves better than two rushed ones.
Source & tradition
Traditional practice; the Catechism treats contrition and confession at CCC 1451-1498.
Spiritual fruit
The sacrament's own grace: forgiveness and peace, received through the Church, not through the prayer alone.

Scripture for Prayer

Psalm 22 - The Lord Is My Shepherd (Douay-Rheims numbering: Psalm 22)

"The Lord ruleth me: and I shall want nothing. He hath set me in a place of pasture. He hath brought me up, on the water of refreshment: he hath converted my soul. He hath led me on the paths of justice, for his own name's sake. For though I should walk in the midst of the shadow of death, I will fear no evils, for thou art with me. Thy rod and thy staff, they have comforted me."

Purpose
Trust when the path is dark: God's guidance through uncertainty, illness, and death.
How to pray
As lectio divina: read it through, then again one verse at a time, resting on the verse that stops you.
When & how long
In uncertainty and illness, and at funerals; five unhurried minutes.
Source & tradition
Psalm 22 (23 in most modern Bibles), public-domain Douay-Rheims Bible (Challoner revision).
Spiritual fruit
Trust: the psalm does not deny the shadow; it denies the aloneness inside it.
Promises & status
Church-attested: reading Scripture with reverence carries a partial indulgence, and a plenary indulgence when done for at least half an hour, under the usual conditions (Enchiridion Indulgentiarum, Apostolic Penitentiary).

The Beatitudes (Matthew 5:3-5)

"Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are the meek: for they shall possess the land. Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted."

Purpose
To let Christ's own definition of happiness correct ours.
How to pray
Read slowly and stop at the beatitude that resists you; that one is yours to carry today.
When & how long
Any season, especially when priorities feel scrambled; five minutes.
Source & tradition
Matthew 5:3-5, public-domain Douay-Rheims Bible (Challoner revision).
Spiritual fruit
The promises named are Christ's own, kingdom-shaped rather than circumstance-shaped: comfort, inheritance, mercy.
Promises & status
Church-attested: as above, devout Scripture reading carries a partial indulgence, plenary for half an hour or more under the usual conditions (Enchiridion Indulgentiarum).

For Someone You Love

Saint Charbel Prayer for the Sick

When someone you love is sick, you learn a new kind of helplessness - and a new kind of prayer. These prayers are for that place: for the hospital waiting room, the night before surgery, the long haul of chronic illness. Pray them by name, and do not carry the sick person alone.

A Prayer for a Sick Loved One

The prayers on this page are original compositions of this website, written in the tradition of the Church's prayers of intercession.

"Saint Charbel, I love someone who is suffering, and I cannot take their place. (Name them) is sick, and I am asking you to stand where I cannot stand - at their side, before God. Ask the Lord to ease their pain, to guide every hand that treats them, to give them sleep tonight and courage tomorrow. Hold them when I am not there, and hold me too, because loving a sick person is its own kind of tired. Amen."

Before Surgery or Treatment

"Saint Charbel, tomorrow they carry (name them) into a room I cannot enter. Go in with them. Ask the Lord to steady the surgeon's hands, to make the treatment work, to keep fear far from their heart. You spent your life in the presence of God - bring that presence into that room, and bring them safely back to us. Amen."

In Chronic Illness

"Saint Charbel, this illness does not end in a day, and neither does the loving. Give (name them) patience for the slow road: strength for the bad days, small joys on the good ones, and people who do not drift away. Teach me to keep showing up without pretending I am not afraid. And Lord, if healing is not what You have planned, let there be peace - real peace - in its place. Amen."

How to Pray for Someone's Healing

  • Pray by name, and tell them. Knowing someone is praying for you is itself a medicine the tradition has always believed in.
  • Keep a rhythm. The nine-day novena on a sick person's behalf, or the 22nd of the month, gives the love somewhere to go, day after day.
  • Anoint with the holy oil. If you have oil from Annaya, trace a small cross on their forehead as you pray - a devotional gesture of faith, never a substitute for treatment.
  • Offer a Mass. Having a Mass offered for a sick person is one of the Church's oldest ways of praying - any parish can arrange it.
  • Let the doctors work. Prayer and medicine are allies. Saint Charbel's devotees fill hospitals as well as shrines, and the tradition honors both.

Documented Healings

Two cases this site has covered, both sourced - read them as hope, not as promises:

  • Nohad El Shami (1993). A Lebanese mother of twelve, paralyzed after a stroke, awoke healed on January 22 after dreaming that Saint Charbel operated on her in the night. She kept a monthly pilgrimage of thanksgiving for the rest of her life. (Source: CNEWA.)
  • Dafne Gutierrez (Phoenix, 2016). A young mother who lost her sight recovered it after prayers before Saint Charbel's relic; the physician who headed the examining medical committee says there was no medical explanation. (Source: Zeale News, 2026.)

The monastery at Annaya keeps a register of more than 29,000 recorded favors - see the miracles page for how the Church weighs them, and letters for stories in people's own words.

Frequently Asked Questions

What prayer should I say for a sick person?

Any honest one. This page offers original prayers for a sick loved one, for surgery, and for chronic illness; the traditional Annaya novena prayer is on the miracle-prayer page. Name the person, name the illness, ask plainly.

Is praying for the sick different from the prayer for healing page?

They overlap, as love does. The healing page centers the prayer itself and the tradition's promises; this page is for specific situations - someone you love, surgery, chronic illness.

Should the sick person pray too, or only others?

Both. The prayer-for-healing page has a prayer written for the one who is sick themselves; this page arms the people who love them.

Can I ask for a Mass to be offered for someone sick?

Yes - one of the Church's oldest customs. Ask any parish; a small offering is customary but never a condition.

What if the person does not get better?

Then you have not failed, and neither has the prayer. The tradition promises that prayer is heard, not that every illness ends in cure - the miracles page and the 'when the miracle does not come' section on the miracle-prayer page face that honestly.

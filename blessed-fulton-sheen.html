Blessed Fulton Sheen

He preached to millions in their living rooms and won an Emmy doing it - a bishop with a blackboard who made the Gospel make sense on television. On September 24, 2026, in Saint Louis, Missouri, the Church declared him Blessed. He is not yet a saint: beatification is the step before canonization, and his cause continues. This page is his story, what the declaration means, and where to hear the man himself.

At a Glance

Who: Fulton J. Sheen (1895-1979), American archbishop, preacher, and pioneer of religious broadcasting.

Known for: the television program Life Is Worth Living (1952-1957), watched by millions, and an Emmy Award in 1953.

Status: declared Blessed on September 24, 2026, in Saint Louis, Missouri - beatified, not yet canonized. He is not "Saint Fulton Sheen," and this page will not call him one.

The miracle: the revival of James Fulton Engstrom, stillborn in 2010, attributed to Sheen's intercession and formally recognized as the miracle for his beatification.

His Life (1895-1979)

Peter John Sheen was born in 1895 in El Paso, Illinois, and raised in a devout Catholic family - as a boy he was known affectionately as "Uncle Fultie." After studies in Rome, he was ordained a priest for the Diocese of Peoria and went on to further study in Washington and at the University of Louvain in Belgium, one of Europe's great centers of philosophy.

He became a professor, a prolific author, and above all a preacher - first to the nation over radio's The Catholic Hour, beginning in 1930, and then to a television audience no religious broadcaster had reached before. In 1966 Pope Paul VI appointed him Bishop of Rochester, New York; he resigned the diocese three years later and continued his public ministry until his death in December 1979. He was buried in St Patrick's Cathedral in New York; in 2019 his remains were brought home to Peoria, the diocese that had shepherded his cause.

The Media Ministry: a Bishop With a Blackboard

Life Is Worth Living aired from 1952 to 1957 and drew millions of viewers every week - an audience that cut across denominations in an era when television was new and its rules unwritten. Sheen's props were a blackboard and a piece of chalk; his method was humor, story, and a scholar's clarity worn lightly. In 1953 he won the Emmy Award for Most Outstanding Personality, accepting it with a nod to his "writers" - Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John.

The day before the beatification, Pope Leo XIV held up exactly this legacy at the Wednesday General Audience: "May the example and intercession of this holy bishop, known for his evangelizing work through the media, inspire many others likewise to find new ways of spreading the Gospel message with joyful enthusiasm."

The Path to the Altars

The Church's process moved in documented steps. In 2012 Pope Benedict XVI recognized his heroic virtue and declared him Venerable. In 2019 Pope Francis authorized the decree recognizing a miracle through his intercession: the revival of James Fulton Engstrom, stillborn in 2010, whose recovery after prayer for Sheen's intercession had no medical explanation the Church's examiners could find. That recognition cleared him for beatification.

On September 24, 2026, Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle, Pro-Prefect of the Dicastery for Evangelization, presided at the beatification Mass in The Dome at America's Center in Saint Louis, Missouri, and proclaimed Pope Leo XIV's apostolic letter declaring him Blessed. Organizers put attendance near 50,000, with some 90 bishops and a thousand priests concelebrating. James Fulton Engstrom himself, now a teenager, carried Sheen's relic - his jawbone, chosen to honor the gifts of the preacher - in the procession.

What Beatification Means - and What It Does Not

Beatification is the Church's formal judgment that a person is in heaven and may be venerated - with the title Blessed - ordinarily within particular places or communities. Canonization, which makes someone a saint of the universal Church, is a further step that ordinarily requires a second miracle after beatification. The same honesty this site keeps between a healing recorded at Annaya and one formally recognized by the Church applies here: Fulton Sheen is Blessed, his cause for canonization is ongoing, and calling him a saint today would simply be wrong.

What beatification does do is propose him as a model and intercessor - above all for everyone who works in media, teaches, or preaches: proof that the new means of communication can carry the old Gospel without losing it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Fulton Sheen a saint?

Not yet. He was beatified on September 24, 2026, and carries the title Blessed. Canonization - becoming a saint of the universal Church - ordinarily requires a second miracle recognized after beatification. His cause is ongoing.

When and where was he beatified?

On September 24, 2026, in The Dome at America's Center in Saint Louis, Missouri. Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle presided, representing Pope Leo XIV, before a crowd organizers estimated near 50,000.

What miracle was recognized for his beatification?

The revival of James Fulton Engstrom, stillborn in 2010, after his family prayed for Sheen's intercession. Pope Francis authorized the decree recognizing the miracle in 2019; Engstrom carried Sheen's relic in the beatification procession.

Why is he called the media bishop?

He preached on national radio from 1930 and on television's Life Is Worth Living from 1952 to 1957, reaching millions weekly and winning an Emmy in 1953. Pope Leo XIV praised this media apostolate at the General Audience the day before the beatification.

Where can I watch his talks?

Five short talks and six full-length lectures are embedded above, and the official archive - full episodes and hundreds of recordings - is at the Fulton Sheen Foundation's video and audio library (fultonsheen.com).

Where is he buried?

His remains rest in Peoria, Illinois, his home diocese, after being transferred there from St Patrick's Cathedral in New York in 2019.

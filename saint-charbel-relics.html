The Relics

Saint Charbel's Relics: From Annaya to the World

Bone fragments from Saint Charbel's tomb travel the world in hand-carved reliquaries - from Maronite parishes across America to cathedrals in Mexico - while his remains rest at Annaya. What the relics are, where they have gone, and what the Church means by venerating them.

The Tomb Story Devotion Worldwide Visit Annaya

What the Relics Are

When Saint Charbel's grave was opened in 1976, after decades in which the body remained remarkably preserved, only the skeleton remained - the full chronology is on the tomb page. From those remains come the relics: bone fragments, in Catholic terms first-class relics, kept at the monastery of Annaya and shared with churches around the world. Parishes typically receive a small fragment set in a reliquary - the American tour's relics travelled in a hand-carved wooden one.

The 2015-2016 American Tour

The widest journey came for the fiftieth anniversary of his beatification. Beginning in late 2015, first-class relics of Saint Charbel visited all thirty-some Maronite parishes in the United States, along with Roman and Byzantine Catholic parishes - Minneapolis in November 2015, then up and down the East Coast through the spring of 2016: Florida, Georgia, the Carolinas, Virginia, Washington, Pennsylvania, New York, New Jersey, Connecticut, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Maine. Local papers recorded churches full at every stop. Similar visits have continued since - in 2025, Illinois parishes in Vandalia and Springfield hosted a first-class relic brought from Lebanon for public veneration.

Relics Around the World

The same pattern repeats wherever his devotion has spread - and it has spread far beyond Lebanon. Relics anchor the devotion at shrines and parishes across the Americas, Europe, and Australia; Mexico's great San Charbel cathedral in Toluca is only the largest example. The map of that devotion, country by country, is on our Saint Charbel around the world page, and recent events like the Naples oil vessel of July 2025 are on the recent miracles page.

What Veneration Means - and What It Does Not

A clear line matters here, and the Church draws it carefully. Catholics venerate relics; they worship God alone. Veneration - dulia, in the old vocabulary - is honor paid to a saint, a way of drawing near to someone who lived close to God; it is never worship of the object or the person. A relic has no power of its own: like the blessed oil, it is a sacramental, an occasion for prayer and faith, not a charm. The healings people report in the presence of relics belong to the same category as every account on this site: testimonies of prayer answered, recorded with gratitude, never promised outcomes. The Church has also always insisted that relics are never bought or sold - they are given.

The Heart of Them Stays at Annaya

However far the fragments travel, the remains of Saint Charbel rest where the villagers buried him in 1898 - in the crypt of the monastery at Annaya, visited by hundreds of thousands of pilgrims a year, and by Pope Leo XIV in December 2025. The practical guide to praying at the tomb and climbing to the hermitage is on our visit page.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where are Saint Charbel's relics?

His remains rest in the crypt of the Monastery of Saint Maron at Annaya, Lebanon. Bone fragments - first-class relics - have been shared with parishes and shrines around the world, especially Maronite churches.

Did Saint Charbel's relics tour the United States?

Yes. In late 2015 through 2016, for the fiftieth anniversary of his beatification, his first-class relics visited all thirty-some American Maronite parishes plus Roman and Byzantine Catholic parishes, from Minneapolis to the East Coast. Similar visits continue, including Illinois stops in 2025.

What is a first-class relic?

In Catholic custom, a first-class relic is a part of a saint's body - in Saint Charbel's case, bone fragments from his remains. Second-class relics are objects he used; third-class relics are objects touched to a first-class relic or to his tomb.

Do Catholics worship relics?

No. The Church distinguishes veneration - honor shown to saints and their relics - from worship, which belongs to God alone. A relic is a sacramental: an occasion for prayer, with no power of its own.

Can you buy a relic of Saint Charbel?

No - and you should refuse anyone selling one. Church law forbids the sale of sacred relics; genuine relics are given to churches, not sold to collectors. The monastery at Annaya and established Maronite parishes are the trustworthy channels.

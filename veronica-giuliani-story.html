The Story of Saint Veronica Giuliani

A true story told in simple words: the girl from Mercatello who became a Poor Clare sister, wrote a Diary of 22,000 pages, and showed that love is something you build.

Short answer: This read-aloud Saint Veronica Giuliani storybook for children ages 5 to 12 tells how Orsola of Mercatello became Sister Veronica of Città di Castello, a Capuchin Poor Clare who spent fifty years in one monastery, guided new sisters for thirty-four years, wrote a Diary of about 22,000 handwritten pages, and served as abbess, even laying water pipes for her sisters.

    About Saint Veronica Giuliani

    Lived
    1660-1727
    Place
    Mercatello, then Città di Castello, Italy
    Status
    Canonized May 26, 1839, by Pope Gregory XVI
    Feast
    July 9

    Orsola Giuliani was born on December 27, 1660, in Mercatello, in Italy's Metauro Valley. At seventeen she entered the monastery of the Capuchin Poor Clares in Città di Castello and received the name Veronica, meaning "true image." She spent fifty years there: thirty-four as novice mistress, and from 1716 as abbess. On obedience she wrote a Diary of about 22,000 handwritten pages. She died on July 9, 1727, and was canonized by Pope Gregory XVI in 1839.

    About this story

    These scenes are imaginative illustrations, not historical footage. The mystical experiences are presented as the Church's old records and Veronica's own Diary report them, not as events other people watched. Veronica's life was unusual. We never hurt our bodies to show love. We show love by praying, helping and being kind. The final page is a "Points of reflection" invitation to pray for someone in need.

    Sources: Benedict XVI, General Audience of 15 December 2010, the Catholic Encyclopedia, and the Enciclopedia Treccani.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Who was Saint Veronica Giuliani?

    Born Orsola Giuliani in Mercatello, Italy, in 1660, she became a Capuchin Poor Clare sister in Città di Castello, took the name Veronica, and spent fifty years in that monastery. She wrote a Diary of about 22,000 pages and was canonized in 1839. Her feast day is July 9.

    Who is this storybook for?

    Children ages 5 to 12, reading on their own or listening with family. The last page offers a question, a Bible passage, a small action and a prayer to share.

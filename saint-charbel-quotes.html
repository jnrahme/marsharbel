The Saint of Silence: Sources and Attribution

Saint Charbel Quotes

Words attributed to Saint Charbel reach readers through different kinds of sources. A published collection, a remembered saying and a reported spiritual experience are not the same kind of evidence. This page identifies the source of each selection without presenting later reports as writings preserved from his lifetime.

Where These Selections Come From

The Family of Saint Sharbel presents Raymond Nader as a Maronite Christian and publishes his account alongside a series of spiritual messages. We summarize these as his reported experiences, not as independently established supernatural events or Church-recognized miracles.

Love Is a Radiant Light: The Life and Words of St. Charbel (Hannah Skandar, Angelico Press) is a separate published collection. Quotations drawn from it are labeled with that book's title; its publication is not evidence that the messages in the other source survive in the saint's own handwriting.

Words Reported Through Others

Some of the most repeated "Saint Charbel quotes" were not written by him at all - they are words others reported hearing from him, and they belong to testimony, not to his writings. The most famous comes from Nohad El Shami, the Lebanese mother of twelve whose healing in January 1993 began the 22nd-of-the-month pilgrimage: she recounted that Saint Charbel came to her in a dream and told her, "I have come to operate on you." That sentence is her testimony - treasured by the monastery and retold everywhere - and we present it as such, not as a written saying of the saint.

The same is true of his death. Biographies record that he died on Christmas Eve 1898 while celebrating Mass, having been struck by illness days earlier at the altar, and they hand down his last recorded offering - that he had given everything to the monastery and now gave his soul to Christ. These are the community's remembered words about his death, not sentences from his pen.

How to Spot a Misattributed Quote

Search the internet for Saint Charbel quotes and you will find long lists - many beautiful, most unsourced, some written by other saints or by modern authors. Before sharing one, ask two questions:

  • Does it appear in a traceable collection? Identify the publication, the exact passage and its kind of evidence. A booklet or published book can establish where a line appears without proving that Saint Charbel wrote it during his lifetime.
  • Is it presented as testimony? A reported experience must name its witness and source. It is not a surviving writing by the saint or independent verification of the reported event.

Reverence includes care with attribution. Share a line with its source and context, and keep later reported experiences distinct from evidence about the saint's lifetime.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are Saint Charbel's most famous quotes?

This page includes selections from Love Is a Radiant Light and the Words of Saint Charbel booklet. The latter messages are attributed through Raymond Nader's reported spiritual experiences, not treated as surviving writings from Saint Charbel's lifetime.

Did Saint Charbel write any books?

The sources cited here do not establish a book written and published by Saint Charbel. The Words of Saint Charbel messages must be identified as attributed messages in Raymond Nader's reported spiritual experiences; Love Is a Radiant Light is a later published collection.

Did Saint Charbel say "I have come to operate on you"?

That sentence is testimony, not a written saying. Nohad El Shami recounted that Saint Charbel spoke it to her in a dream before her reported healing in January 1993 - the event that began the 22nd-of-the-month pilgrimage. It is treasured by the monastery, and we present it as her testimony, not as the saint's own writing.

How can you tell if a Saint Charbel quote is real?

Check the exact wording, publication and context. Separate a later reported experience from a remembered saying or a surviving writing. A traceable source establishes the attribution being made, not independent proof that the saint spoke or wrote those words.

The Saint of Silence, In His Own Words

Saint Charbel Quotes

People search for the quotes of Saint Charbel expecting volumes of sayings. The honest answer surprises them: the hermit of Annaya wrote almost nothing down, gave no recorded sermons, and chose silence as his life's language. What survives is a small body of homilies and sayings preserved by his monastic family - and it is precious precisely because it is small. Here is what we actually have, where it comes from, and a short selection with sources.

The Sourced Selection His Life Story Become Like Him

What Survives, and Where It Comes From

Saint Charbel spent his last twenty-three years in near-total silence at the hermitage above Annaya. He kept no diary and published nothing. The words that survive come to us through his brothers in the Lebanese Maronite Order, who preserved a handful of short texts and remembered sayings after his death.

Two collections carry nearly everything we have. The first is the booklet tradition usually titled "Words of Saint Charbel", which presents the small set of spiritual messages that remain from his pen - its own contents page describes them as the surviving messages of his words. The second is "Love Is a Radiant Light: The Life and Words of St. Charbel" (Hannah Skandar, Angelico Press), an English collection of his homilies and proverbs with a short biography, whose translator compares them to the sayings of the Desert Fathers: simple, direct, and shining.

Everything this page quotes is drawn from those two traditions. Where a saying cannot be traced to them, we say so rather than repeat it.

Words Reported Through Others

Some of the most repeated "Saint Charbel quotes" were not written by him at all - they are words others reported hearing from him, and they belong to testimony, not to his writings. The most famous comes from Nohad El Shami, the Lebanese mother of twelve whose healing in January 1993 began the 22nd-of-the-month pilgrimage: she recounted that Saint Charbel came to her in a dream and told her, "I have come to operate on you." That sentence is her testimony - treasured by the monastery and retold everywhere - and we present it as such, not as a written saying of the saint.

The same is true of his death. Biographies record that he died on Christmas Eve 1898 while celebrating Mass, having been struck by illness days earlier at the altar, and they hand down his last recorded offering - that he had given everything to the monastery and now gave his soul to Christ. These are the community's remembered words about his death, not sentences from his pen.

How to Spot a Misattributed Quote

Search the internet for Saint Charbel quotes and you will find long lists - many beautiful, most unsourced, some written by other saints or by modern authors. Before sharing one, ask two questions:

  • Does it appear in a traceable collection? The Skandar book (Angelico Press) or the "Words of Saint Charbel" booklet tradition are the reference points. If a quote appears in neither, treat it as unverified.
  • Is it presented as testimony? Words like Nohad El Shami's are real - but they are reported experiences, clearly labeled, not the saint's writings.

A saint who chose silence for twenty-three years deserves better than words put in his mouth. The few sayings we can verify are enough - they carried a hermit through a lifetime, and they hold up under a lifetime of rereading.
